## Abstract

Purpose – This study compares how Korean broadcast and newspaper news framed the 2026 Iran War, focusing on whether the conflict was represented mainly as a security and diplomatic issue, or as a structural supply chain risk linked to the Strait of Hormuz, energy flows, maritime logistics, and economic security.
Design/Methodology/Approach – The study collected online news articles published between February 28th and April 19th, 2026, from three major broadcasters (KBS, MBC, and SBS) and three national newspapers (The Chosun Daily, Korea JoongAng Daily, and HANKYOREH). After data refinement, 5,331 broadcast news articles and 4,981 newspaper articles were analyzed. Keyword network analysis and LDA topic modeling were conducted using NetMiner to identify core keywords, centrality structures, and latent topic patterns.
Findings – Both media types framed the Iran War as an international crisis centered on the United States, Israel, and the Strait of Hormuz. However, broadcast news emphasized military tension, diplomatic responses, and security-centered event reporting. Newspaper coverage, in contrast, placed the Strait of Hormuz at the center of the discourse and connected the war more directly to crude oil supply, shipping routes, energy prices, and supply chain instability.
Research Implications – This study extends war-framing research by incorporating economic security and supply chain risk perspectives. It also suggests that news discourse can serve as an early signal for firms and policymakers seeking to monitor geopolitical risks and prepare supply chain responses.
