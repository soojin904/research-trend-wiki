# Consumer adoption of microgreens in a sustainable urban food system: awareness, willingness to pay, and barriers in the UAE

## 초록

Background Microgreens are young edible seedlings cultivated in controlled settings using less water, land, and external inputs, and are increasingly recognized as a resource-efficient crop aligned with the aims of sustainable urban food systems. In countries like the UAE—an arid environment that relies heavily on imports, where local agricultural production accounts for only a small share of total caloric intake—locally produced short-supply-chain crops such as microgreens offer structural advantages within the food system. Although the UAE has made substantial investments in vertical farming and controlled-environment agriculture, empirical research on consumer awareness and adoption intentions remains limited. Methods A cross-sectional online survey ( N = 188) was conducted among UAE residents from May 2024 to April 2025 using convenience sampling. Descriptive statistics, chi-square tests with effect sizes, and Firth's penalized logistic regression were used to examine associations between information sources, perceived barriers, and key adoption outcomes. The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) was applied as an interpretive lens rather than a formally tested model. The level of interest in learning about microgreens was high (91.5%), and there was an intention of consumption among 70.2% of respondents. Willingness to accept a premium price for microgreens, considered here as an indicator of latent demand rather than an economic value, had been noted in 74.7%, and this was significantly correlated with the intention to consume [ <mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="M2"> <mml:msubsup> <mml:mrow> <mml:mi>χ</mml:mi> </mml:mrow> <mml:mrow> <mml:mrow> <mml:mo stretchy="false">(</mml:mo> <mml:mrow> <mml:mn>1</mml:mn> </mml:mrow> <mml:mo stretchy="false">)</mml:mo> </mml:mrow> </mml:mrow> <mml:mrow> <mml:mn>2</mml:mn> </mml:mrow> </mml:msubsup> </mml:math> = 11.33, p &amp;lt; 0.001, ϕ = 0.247]. Health-benefit awareness [OR = 5.42, 95% CI (1.69, 20.10), p = 0.004] and retail visibility [OR = 2.43, 95% CI (1.06, 5.74), p = 0.036] were significantly associated with consideration of home cultivation in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. Conclusions In this exploratory convenience sample, informational gaps, rather than clear attitudinal resistance or stated price-premium rejection, appeared to be associated with lower microgreens adoption intentions in the UAE. The findings suggest that consumer education and improved retail visibility may complement investments in controlled-environment agriculture. The observed interest and stated price-premium acceptance provide preliminary consumer-side evidence relevant to the UAE National Food Security Strategy 2051 and SDGs 2 and 12; however, the study does not measure actual purchasing behavior, market demand, or environmental outcomes.
