# Prevalence of Selfitis Behaviour Among College Students

## 초록

Background Selfitis behaviour is an emerging psychosocial concern among young adults, characterized by selfie addiction and sharing, often associated with the need for social approval, self-presentation, and emotional regulation.It is an emerging problem among adolescents as the misuse of social media leads to increased life-threatening consequences. Thepresent study was intended to assess the level of selfitis behaviour among college students. The objectives of the study were to assess the prevalence of selfitis behaviour among college students and find out the association between selfitis with selected socio demographic variables. In this study, the sample were Bsc Nursing students fromSivagiri Sree Narayana Medical Mission College of Nursing, Varkala. Structured questionnaire was used to assess the level of selfitis behaviour and socio-demographic variables. A Standardized Selfitis Behaviour Scale was used to assess the level of selfitis behaviour. The research approach and design used were non-experimental quantitative approach and descriptive research design respectively. Out of 110 samples, 46% of young adults have borderline selfitis, representing the majority of the study. Furthermore, 25% of young adults have acute selfitis and an equal 25% have no selfitis, while the remaining 4% of young adults have chronic selfitis. The prevalence of selfitis behaviour among young adults is associated with the education of mother, monthly family income and number of selfies taken per day. However, no significant association was found with variables such as age, sex, religion, type of family, semester of study, or preferred social media platform. Methodology Research approach In this study quantitative non-experimental approach was used to assess the prevalence of selfitis behaviour among college students of Sivagiri Sree Narayana Medical Mission College Of Nursing, Varkala, Thiruvananthapuram. Results The results show that out of 110 samples, 46% of young adults have borderline selfitis, representing the majority of the study. Furthermore, 25% of young adults have acute selfitis and an equal 25% have no selfitis, while the remaining 4% of young adults have chronic selfitis. The chi-square value shows that there was statistically significant association between the prevalence of selfitis behaviour and education of mother (p < 0.05), monthly family income (p < 0.05) and number of selfies taken per day (p < 0.001) among college students. No significant association between the prevalence of selfitis behaviour and, sex, religion, type of family, semester, education of father, occupation of mother, occupation of father, area of residence, type of accommodation, duration of daily usage of phone, duration of smartphone use in years, preferred social media platform for posting selfies taken ( p> 0.05) among college students. Conclusion Selfitis behaviour is an emerging psychosocial concern among young adults, characterized by excessive selfie-taking and sharing, often associated with the need for social approval, self-presentation, and emotional regulation.According to the study findings the following conclusions were derived. Out of 110 samples, 46% of young adults have borderline selfitis, representing the majority of the study. Furthermore, 25% of young adults have acute selfitis and an equal 25% have no selfitis, while the remaining 4% of young adults have chronic selfitis. The prevalence of selfitis behaviour among college students is associated with the education of mother, monthly family income and number of selfies taken per day. A self structured questionnaire and Selfitis Behaviour Scale was effective in assessing the prevalence of selfitis behaviour among college students.
