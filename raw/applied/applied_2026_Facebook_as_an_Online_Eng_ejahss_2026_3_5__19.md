# Facebook as an Online English Language Learning Platform: A Case Study of a Provincial Public University in Cambodia

**저자**: Riem Meng; Chiva Chin; Sokheng Hon; Sreykeo Peng; Rany Sam
**출처**: European Journal of Arts Humanities and Social Sciences, Vol.3 No.5, pp.223-241
**발행일**: 2026-10-10
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.59324/ejahss.2026.3(5).19
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Facebook remains a widely used social networking platform with communication features that also support informal and supplementary language learning. This quantitative case study examined how undergraduate students at the National University of Battambang use Facebook and how they perceive its value for learning English. A questionnaire adapted from Kabilan et al. (2010) was administered to 250 students in Years 2–4. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize Facebook use patterns, English-related activities, and perceptions; Welch’s t test and one-way ANOVA were used to examine differences by gender and academic year, and an exploratory ANOVA was used to examine differences by faculty. The participants reported an overall positive perception of Facebook as an English-learning environment (M = 3.65, SD = 0.48, α = .87). Vocabulary learning received the highest item mean (M = 4.10, SD = 0.72), whereas confidence in speaking with other English learners received the lowest (M = 3.26, SD = 0.94). Perceptions did not differ significantly by sex (Welch’s t(68.19) = 0.52, p = .605, d = 0.09) or academic year (F(2, 247) = 1.31, p = .271, η² = .011). However, perceptions differed by faculty (F(3, 246) = 8.56, p < .001, η² = .094), with Institute of Foreign Languages students reporting the most favorable views. Mixed Khmer–English was the most common language choice on Facebook, and more frequent use of Facebook for learning was modestly associated with more positive perceptions (Spearman’s ρ = .19, p = .003). The findings suggest that Facebook is best treated as a supplementary environment for exposure, interaction, and learner choice rather than as a substitute for structured instruction. The study recommends purposeful integration of Facebook activities with explicit learning objectives, structured speaking tasks, and outcome measures.
