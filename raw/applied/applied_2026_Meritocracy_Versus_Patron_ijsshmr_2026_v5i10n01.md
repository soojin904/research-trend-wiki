# Meritocracy Versus Patronage: The Impact of Political Connections on Administrative Efficiency in Nigeria's Public Service

## 초록

The debate between meritocracy and political patronage remains a central concern in public administration, particularly in developing democracies where political considerations often shape bureaucratic processes. This study examines the impact of political connections on administrative efficiency in Nigeria's public service through a qualitative desk review approach. Anchored on Weberian Bureaucratic Theory, the study investigates how merit-based administrative principles and patronage-driven practices influence bureaucratic performance, service delivery, and institutional effectiveness. Adopting a qualitative desk review approach, the study synthesizes evidence from peer-reviewed journal articles, policy reports, and governance studies focusing on meritocracy, political patronage, recruitment practices, and public sector performance in Nigeria and comparable developing-country contexts. The findings reveal a consistent positive relationship between meritocratic recruitment and administrative efficiency. Merit-based systems enhance professionalism, employee commitment, organizational productivity, and policy implementation by ensuring that public positions are occupied by competent and qualified personnel. Conversely, the review identifies political patronage, manifested through clientelism, godfatherism, nepotism, and politically motivated appointments, as a major impediment to bureaucratic effectiveness. The study concludes that strengthening meritocratic principles, enhancing institutional autonomy, promoting transparency in personnel management, and reforming representational policies are essential for improving administrative efficiency in Nigeria's public service. By synthesizing existing evidence and identifying critical institutional challenges, the study contributes to ongoing debates on public sector reform and provides policy insights for building a professional, accountable, and development-oriented bureaucracy.
