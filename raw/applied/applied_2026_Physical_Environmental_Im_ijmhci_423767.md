# Physical Environmental Immersion and Situated Learning

**저자**: Xiaowan Yang; Xiaoyu Guo; Zhenliang Wang

**발행일**: 2026-10-02

**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Individuals may understand environmental regulations yet fail to act on that knowledge, reflecting a persistent knowledge-action gap that conventional classroom instruction rarely addresses. To examine one potential remedy, we conducted a comparative field study in which 150 university students completed paper-based tasks on wetland protection laws either in a natural wetland park or in a classroom setting. The two conditions differed jointly in setting, materials (color photographs with location cues versus plain text), mobility, and sensory exposure, so the results reflect this bundle rather than any single factor. The study measured immediate knowledge mastery, self-reported behavioral intention, a brief hypothetical simulation response, and task time. The wetland group scored 82% versus 65% on knowledge and 76% versus 58% on behavioral intention. These short-term differences suggest that a situated physical context may enhance immediate learning and intention, although actual behavior change and causal attribution remain untested.
