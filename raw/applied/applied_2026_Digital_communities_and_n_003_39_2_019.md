# Digital communities and non-suicidal self-harm (NSSH): young people’s languages on the X social network

**저자**: Miguel Ángel Nicolás Ojeda; Esther Martínez Pastor; Pablo Sánchez López
**출처**: Communication & Society
**발행일**: 2026-09-29
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.15581/003.39.2.019
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

This paper examines non-suicidal self-harm (NSSH) among adolescents and young people from a digital communication perspective, focusing on the textual and visual language used on the social networking platform X. Young users interact by creating profiles and digital communities centered on NSSH, which facilitates the dissemination and consumption of related content. The study employed a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative data collection via an application programming interface (API) with qualitative content analysis. The analysis aimed to identify user profiles, interaction patterns, and the most frequently used texts, images, emojis, videos, hashtags, and keywords. Results show that user gender is identifiable in only 14% of cases, 99% of posts include text, 35% contain images of real people, and 5.3% feature video content. Findings highlight the use of a specific set of hashtags, previously noted in other studies, which label NSSH content and promote its circulation. The visual language employed by young users contributes to a romanticized aesthetic surrounding self-harm, encouraging engagement and shaping user identities within this phenomenon. These profiles, in turn, facilitate the formation of digital communities around NSSH. The widespread presence of NSSH content on the platform indicates that many posts bypass its posting guidelines, underscoring the challenges of moderating such material. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of understanding the language, aesthetics, and interaction dynamics of NSSH content online to inform prevention strategies, provide appropriate support, and promote safer digital environments for young people.
