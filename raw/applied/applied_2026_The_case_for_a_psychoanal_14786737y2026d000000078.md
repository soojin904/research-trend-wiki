# The case for a psychoanalytically and socially literate approach to sexual consent education with young people

## 초록

This article seeks to contribute to efforts to find a meaningful approach to sexual consent education with young people in the UK. It highlights current debates around how to design sexual consent policy and pedagogy in educational institutions and shows how some approaches might insufficiently account for how unconscious psychological and external social forces coalesce to frame young people’s decisions, obscuring the reasons why they are pursuing sexual intimacy. Deploying some empirical data from small-scale qualitative research and Lacanian theory, it suggests that attempts to codify consent through laws and policies such as affirmative consent, and some associated educational practices, can misfire because they expect too much ‘knowingness’ precisely when unknowingness is inseparable from sexuality, especially for young people embarking on sex for the first time. It proposes that pedagogic attempts to minimise the risk of trauma and harm arising from non-consensual sex need therefore to engage in dialogue with young people that is both psychoanalytically and socially literate, be bold, introduced early, and fully account for the limits of language. The article concludes with some implications of the proposed pedagogy for professionals working in relationship and sex education with young people.
