# Why empowerment, not relief? Business networks and the economic resilience of disaster-prone areas households in rural China

**저자**: Zhidong Wu; Junyi Wan
**출처**: Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems, Vol.10
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1932544
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Introduction The “differential mode of association” theory describes a traditional Chinese society centered around personal, family, and local relationships. However, due to market changes, population movement, and networking, these traditional ties are weakening, and business networks are becoming more important. Methods Using data from 2016 to 2022 China Family Panel Studies (CFPS), we investigated the impact of business networks on the economic resilience of disaster-prone areas households in rural China. Results and discussion Results show that business networks enhance the economic resilience of disaster-prone areas households in rural China by providing off-farm employment. Heterogeneity analysis shows that business networks have a double effect, boosting gains for better-off families while still providing crucial support for weaker families to meet basic needs. Further analysis shows that business networks have a significant positive effect on job security and promotion satisfaction, but their impact on income, work environment, and work-life balance is not statistically significant. Conclusion These findings remain robust across various tests, offering new insights into how informal institutions can foster economic resilience of disaster-prone areas households in rural China.
