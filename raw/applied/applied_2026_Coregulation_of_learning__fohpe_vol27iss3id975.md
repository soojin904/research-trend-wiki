# Co-regulation of learning in simulation: A qualitative exploration of experiences, manifestations, enablers and barriers in medical student simulations

**저자**: Natasha Yates; Suzanne Gough; Victoria A Brazil
**출처**: Focus on Health Professional Education A Multi-Professional Journal, Vol.27 No.3, pp.45-67
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.11157/fohpe-vol27iss3id975
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Background: Medical education literature offers limited insights into the presence and potential of co-regulated learning (CoRL), where learners interact reciprocally, particularly in collaborative settings such as simulation-based education (SBE). Our qualitative study explores CoRL within SBE in an undergraduate medical program, describing its manifestations, enablers and barriers. Methods: Employing a constructivist approach and reflexive thematic analysis, we conducted semi-structured interviews with 30 participants across four groups: medical students (n = 13), facilitators (n = 5), simulated patients (n = 6) and embedded simulation faculty (n = 6). Results: From this, we built six interconnected themes: (1) simulation design: considered to optimise CoRL, (2) psychological safety: supports CoRL, (3) student agency and vicarious learning, (4) variable between simulation phases (pre-brief, task, debrief), (5) roles and role modelling: impact on CoRL and (6) learning conversations for CoRL. CoRL was described as most prevalent during task performance, indicating substantial learning occurs within the simulation itself, not just during the debrief. Educators can leverage this through SBE by using approaches such as “pause and discuss”. Learner agency was identified as a critical pre-requisite to CoRL, valued by both students and educators. Educators sought opportunities for students to exhibit their abilities, thereby fostering agency and creating openings for CoRL. Discussion: While generally beneficial, CoRL can sometimes manifest in unhelpful ways. This necessitates educator intervention (through role modelling or direct correction) and requires carefully balancing supporting students while still promoting self-regulated learning. We offer practical suggestions for health profession educators seeking to harness the benefits of CoRL in SBE to enhance collaborative learning and prepare future health professionals to be lifelong learners.
