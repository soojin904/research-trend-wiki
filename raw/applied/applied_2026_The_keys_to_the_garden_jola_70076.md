# The keys to the garden: Capital, class, and the repurposing of Persian mystical poetry in Los Angeles

**저자**: Zia Khoshsirat
**출처**: Journal of Linguistic Anthropology, Vol.36 No.3
**발행일**: 2026-09-28

## 초록

Abstract This article traces the social life of medieval Persian poetry as it is repurposed within Los Angeles's Iranian diaspora. Based on 5 years of immersive ethnography in private erfān classes and mystical assemblies, I argue these gatherings function as contemporary diasporic courts, mobilizing core texts within a spiritual marketplace structured by economic exclusion. While historical scholarship has shown how Persian poetry negotiated status in medieval courts, and anthropological work has grounded these traditions in social life, this study offers the first analysis of how diasporic Iranians monetize this heritage to redraw ethnolinguistic and class boundaries from within. Synthesizing Bourdieu's model of capital conversion with theories of diasporic temporality and the semiotic politics of language, I trace a dual process. Externally, participants leverage poetic mastery to convert economic capital into compound capital of refined civility, positioning the community within Los Angeles's racialized landscape. Internally, through ritualized performances of interpretation and an ideology of pre‐Islamic purity, they forge class boundaries and authenticate a distinct diasporic identity. Ultimately, it reveals how spiritual tradition enables the interconversion of capital through the performance of a purified past.
