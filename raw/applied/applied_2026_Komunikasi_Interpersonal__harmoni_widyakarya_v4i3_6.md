## 초록

The evolution of digital technology has significantly altered communication patterns within romantic relationships. The advent of instant messaging apps, social media, and location-sharing features enables intense interaction, transcending the constraints of space and time. Conversely, this ease of connectivity also creates opportunities for surveillance practices to emerge within the relationship. This study aims to analyze how digital technology-mediated interpersonal communication shapes the dynamics between emotional intimacy and surveillance practices. The research employs a qualitative approach utilizing a reflective autoethnographic method. Data were gathered through the documentation of digital conversations, reflections on personal experiences, and communication artifacts from the daily interactions between the researcher and their partner. The analysis links empirical data with perspectives on digital communication, Uncertainty Reduction Theory, and the concept of the surveillance society. The findings indicate that digital communication serves as a means to establish presence and emotional intimacy between partners. However, the use of features such as location sharing and digital activity monitoring also introduces ambiguity regarding the distinction between care and control. These findings demonstrate that digital technology not only facilitates communication but also reshapes the meanings of trust, transparency, and privacy within romantic relationships.
