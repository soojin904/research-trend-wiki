## 초록

Women’s agency refers to their ability to make choices and act according to their own will. In patriarchal societies, however, this ability is often restricted by social institutions, cultural practices, and expectations that privilege male authority. Jokha Alharthi’s Celestial Bodies brings together the stories of women from different generations and social backgrounds and presents their lives within the patriarchal world of the Omani village of al-Awafi. The paper focuses on Mayya, Asma, Khawla, Najiya, Salima, Zarifa, Ankabuta, Masouda, and London, whose experiences represent different degrees of subordination and self-determination. Their stories demonstrate that agency does not always take the form of direct opposition to patriarchal authority. It may appear in a suppressed desire, a personal choice, a refusal to accept an imposed relationship, an attempt to preserve independence, or the decision to leave an oppressive marriage. The experiences of slave women add another dimension to the discussion by revealing how slavery and patriarchy intersect to deprive women of control over their bodies and lives. At the same time, their resistance illustrates that submission is not the only possibility available to them. This paper attempts to explore how Alharthi’s Celestial Bodies represents women’s agency through their acts of resistance, negotiation, and self-determination, revealing the possibilities of choice and voice that emerge within the constraints of a patriarchal society.
