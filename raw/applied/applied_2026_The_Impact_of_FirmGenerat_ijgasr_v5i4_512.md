# The Impact of Firm-Generated and User-Generated Content on Consumer Buying Behaviour: An Analysis with Reference to Electronics Industry

**저자**: Vinay Babbar; Bharat Bhushan; Ravi Vashisht
**출처**: International Journal for Global Academic & Scientific Research, Vol.5 No.4, pp.85-103
**발행일**: 2026-10-10
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.55938/ijgasr.v5i4.512
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

These days social media have become a primary source of information for the consumers which they consider while making their buying decisions. At present, consumers are getting first-hand information through social media from the direct posts of the firms and via feedbacks and reviews of different users. The wider reach and growing influence of social media are affecting consumers' evaluation and purchase decisions across various product categories. Recognising the growing impact of social media, this study tries to examine the association of user-generated content (UGC) and firm-generated content (FGC) with consumer buying behaviour (CBB) on social media in context of electronic products in India. The study also makes a formal comparison of the relative strength of the FGC and UGC associations with consumer buying behaviour. The primary data were collected by floating a structured questionnaire with a five-point Likert scale, and the 175 respondents were found eligible, for further analysis. Data was analysed using Partial least square structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM). The findings show that both FGC and UGC are positively and significantly associated with CBB, with path coefficients β = 0.274 and β = 0.400 respectively. The numerical coefficient for UGC is larger than the coefficient for FGC, but the bootstrap comparison of the two coefficients resulted in a 95% confidence interval of [-0.169, 0.365], which contains 0. Consequently, the results do not give enough statistical evidence to conclude that UGC has more substantial association with CBB than FGC. The model explains 32.7% of the variance of consumer buying behaviour. The results thus confirm the significance of both content sources, and the results do not allow us to assume that, just because the coefficient is bigger, UGC is more strongly associated with CBB than FGC. Hence, the more likely explanation is that both types of content are linked to consumers' buying intentions, but that there is no statistically meaningful difference between the strengths of these two associations. The results also offer some implications to marketers of electronic products in creating a balanced social media strategy that takes into consideration both firm-initiated and user-initiated content.
