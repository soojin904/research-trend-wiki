# The Resilience of Women-owned Coastal MSMEs Through Social Access, Gender Roles, and Community Capital Among Three Ethnic Groups in Indonesia

## 초록

Women-owned micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) play an important role in supporting household livelihoods and local economic development in Indonesia’s coastal communities. However, these enterprises face challenges related to limited access to financial resources, digital technologies, and market opportunities, which may affect their resilience. This study examines the resilience of womenowned coastal MSMEs through the perspectives of social access, gender roles, and community capital across three ethnic groups in Indonesia: Malay, Javanese, and Minang. A mixed-methods approach was employed using a survey of 144 female MSME owners, complemented by in-depth interviews and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs). The findings reveal that women are the primary actors in coastal MSMEs, demonstrating dominant involvement in production, marketing, innovation, and business management activities. At the same time, women continue to bear substantial domestic responsibilities, reflecting a persistent double burden within coastal households. Access to social networks, training opportunities, and community support contributes significantly to business resilience, while limited digital capabilities and access to formal financing remain major constraints. The findings further indicate that social cohesion, mutual assistance, and community-based networks strengthen both individual and community resilience, enabling female entrepreneurs to adapt to economic and environmental challenges. Differences in social and cultural structures among ethnic groups also influence women’s participation and decision-making. These findings highlight the importance of culturally sensitive empowerment policies that strengthen women’s access to resources, digital capabilities, and community capital to enhance the long-term resilience of coastal MSMEs.
