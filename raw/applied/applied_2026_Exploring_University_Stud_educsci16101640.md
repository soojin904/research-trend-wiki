# Exploring University Students’ Experiences of Using Conversational Artificial Intelligence for Emotional and Relational Support in Experiences of Loneliness

**저자**: Yanyan Li; Tom Ritchie
**출처**: Education Sciences, Vol.16 No.10, pp.1640-1640
**발행일**: 2026-10-03
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16101640
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

University students’ loneliness has remained a persistent concern in the years following the COVID-19 pandemic and correlates with multiple mental health issues, yet little is known about how university students experiencing loneliness use conversational artificial intelligence (CAI) for emotional and relational support. This exploratory qualitative study addressed that gap by exploring how students in a UK university experiencing loneliness perceived their use of CAI for emotional and relational support, and how they positioned CAI alongside other coping strategies. Sixteen students scoring 3 or above on the De Jong Gierveld Loneliness Scale participated in semi-structured interviews. Data were analysed using reflexive thematic analysis. Situating loneliness within students’ wider social and relational lives at university, four themes were developed: CAI as a low-barrier emotional outlet, free from the social costs of human disclosure; a perceived objective reflective sense-making partner that helped organise overwhelming experiences; a pragmatic response to gaps in existing support networks; and a supplement rather than a permanent substitute for human care, approached with critical awareness of its limitations. Students’ use of CAI appeared to reflect unmet relational needs within their existing social networks, rather than an absence of social contact or a preference for AI over people. The study concludes by discussing implications for sector-wide institutional guidance on the responsible governance of AI in relation to student mental health and calls on universities to engage more openly with how AI is shaping students’ lives beyond academic work.
