## 초록

Big data analytics has emerged as a transformative approach in healthcare, offering opportunities to improve clinical decision-making, patient outcomes, operational efficiency, and healthcare delivery. The rapid generation of healthcare data from electronic health records, medical imaging, wearable devices, genomic studies, and digital health platforms has created a need for advanced analytical methods capable of processing complex and heterogeneous datasets. This systematic review aims to examine the applications, benefits, challenges, and future prospects of big data analytics in healthcare. Relevant literature is systematically evaluated to explore the role of descriptive, predictive, prescriptive, and artificial intelligence-driven analytics in disease diagnosis, personalized medicine, patient monitoring, clinical decision support, and healthcare resource management. The review also investigates critical barriers, including data privacy, cybersecurity, interoperability, data quality, ethical concerns, and the shortage of skilled professionals. Furthermore, the integration of machine learning, cloud computing, Internet of Things technologies, and real-time analytics is discussed in relation to enhancing healthcare intelligence and improving patient-centered care. Particular attention is given to the importance of robust data governance, regulatory compliance, and validation of analytical models to ensure safe and equitable implementation. The findings are expected to provide a comprehensive understanding of the current healthcare big data landscape and identify research gaps requiring further investigation. Overall, big data analytics demonstrates substantial potential to support evidence-based healthcare and improve system sustainability. However, successful adoption requires interdisciplinary collaboration, technological innovation, standardized data frameworks, and responsible management of healthcare information to translate analytical insights into reliable clinical and public health outcomes.
