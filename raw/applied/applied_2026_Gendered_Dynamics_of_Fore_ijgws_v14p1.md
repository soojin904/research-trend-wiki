# Gendered Dynamics of Foreign Policy on the Presidential Campaign Trail

**저자**: James Wright
**출처**: INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL OF GENDER & WOMEN S STUDIES
**발행일**: 2026-07-01

## 초록

Due to the small number of candidates and few opportunities provided, little is actually known about the gendered dynamics of campaigning for the US presidency. Hillary Clinton was the first ‘serious’ candidate for a major party nomination in the United States in 2008 and she was the first to win that nomination in 2016. The 2020 Democratic presidential primary campaign provides us the largest single-election sample of women candidates for president, to-date. Using a few text analysis methods, I analyze campaign speech data of the candidates for the Democratic Party’s nomination in 2020 to ascertain whether there exists a gendered difference in the focus placed on foreign policy and domestic policy. I hypothesize that women candidates would employ counterstereotypic strategies that cause them to emphasize foreign policy at a greater rate than their men opponents. The results confirm this hypothesis. Women candidates focused more on foreign policy than their men opponents and gave more attention to foreign policy as it pertains to warfare than their men counterparts. The paper concludes with a discussion about the importance of this finding and the need for further research to be done on the gendered dynamics of presidential campaigning.
