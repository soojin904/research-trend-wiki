# Dialogic Loops or Dialogic Engagement? Addressing Human Rights and Inclusion in Welfare Professions

## 초록

Aim. This article explores how social work professionals and pedagogues negotiate tensions arising from paradigm shifts in their fields. The following objectives have been set: (a) to apply the dialogic communication theory to analyse professional practices in child welfare, inclusive education, and prison social work; (b) to explore how social care professionals apply dialogic principles of mutuality, propinquity, empathy, risk, and commitment; (c) to examine the cultural and structural constraints that hinder professional dialogue during ongoing transformations, creating dialogic loops, and how welfare workers navigate these tensions. Methods. Five focus groups with 25 participants from the fields of child welfare, prison social work and inclusive education were conducted. We used vignettes to initiate conversations with the interviewees, presenting realistic situations from the practical fields and stimulating real-life contextual reasoning. We employed thematic analysis, examining themes through the theoretical lenses of dialogic communication and engagement. Results. Professionals consistently demonstrate their capacity for compassion and emotional engagement with the social welfare clients, by showing mutuality, empathy and commitment. Tenets related to institutional constraints and barriers became apparent, such as a lack of managerial, methodological, financial and other support, institutional reluctance and resistance. Conclusions. The study revealed individual and structural barriers that create dialogic loops, which constrain the dialogic engagement and aspirations of welfare professionals for ‘true’ dialogue. These loops arise from broader public discourse, stigmatisation stemming from societal attitudes, and entrenched barriers within respective welfare systems.
