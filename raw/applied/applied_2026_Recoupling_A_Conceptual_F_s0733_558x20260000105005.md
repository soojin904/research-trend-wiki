## 초록

This essay highlights the overlooked contribution of recoupling studies to our understanding of the ways in which organizations manage external environmental pressures to change. First, we discuss the development of recoupling as a concept and define recoupling as occurring when what was formerly a purely symbolic response becomes a more meaningful implementation of external prescriptions. Second, we detail the dimensions of a conceptual framework for the study of recoupling. As part of this framework, we identify the processes or conditions that make recoupling possible, such as (1) when organizational members internalize the norms and values endorsed by regulation or when new members who espouse external templates join the organization, (2) when there are shifts in organizational power, and/or (3) when complementary templates make norms implementable. We also describe the multi-modal mechanisms through which recoupling unfolds within organizations – that is, discursive forms, material conditions, and relational networks. Third, we illustrate our conceptual argument on recoupling with two longitudinal case studies we have conducted. Overall, our essay provides a framework and directions for enhancing our understanding of a fundamental concept in management, organization, and sociological studies – recoupling.
