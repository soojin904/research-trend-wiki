# Social impacts, ecological effectiveness and long-term governance of ecological protection in Dongting Lake under the background of the 10-Year Fishing Ban on the Yangtze River: a comprehensive analysis based on local social research, project investigation and ecological monitoring data

**저자**: Qiyan Zhang; Yilin Chen; Shu'ao Ma
**출처**: Advances in Social Behavior Research, Vol.17 No.10, pp.110-136
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.54254/2753-7102/2026.37371
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

The 10-Year Fishing Ban on the Yangtze River is not only a conservation system for aquatic biological resources, but also a social practice involving livelihood transformation, interest coordination and grassroots governance. This paper incorporates the social impacts and ecological effectiveness of ecological protection in Dongting Lake into a unified analytical framework, taking national and Hunan provincial policies, the Bulletin on Aquatic Biological Resources and Habitat Conditions in the Yangtze River Basin (2025) and relevant ecological studies as the main evidence, domestic social studies on Dongting Lake as the basis for social analysis, and supplemented by 30 project questionnaires, field observations and official media cases. The study shows that from 2021 to 2025, a total of 112 indigenous fish species were monitored in Dongting Lake; in 2025, the unit resource quantity was 4.4 kg, up 7.3% year on year, and the Shannon-Wiener Diversity Index was 3.0, down 11.8% year on year. A 2022 scientific survey estimated that there were about 162 Yangtze finless porpoises in Dongting Lake; 7 porpoise groups with about 21 individuals including 2 calves were recorded during a specific project period, and this record is not equivalent to a population census. Social studies indicate that stable employment after decommissioning, social security, ecological compensation and policy accessibility are important conditions for long-term recognition. Among the project questionnaires, 46.7% of respondents accurately grasped the starting year of the full fishing ban in key waters, 80.0% agreed with appropriate restrictions on the utilization of some resources, but only 50.0% clearly expressed willingness to take eco-friendly consumption actions, presenting a gap where value recognition is higher than policy knowledge and principle support is higher than action willingness. Comprehensive judgment holds that the 10-year fishing ban has created key conditions for ecological restoration, and the phased achievements should be fully affirmed; however, the growth of resource quantity cannot be equated with the full restoration of the ecosystem. In the second half of the fishing ban period, we should coordinate the monitoring of fish, benthic animals, rare species and key habitats, improve the integrated law enforcement of water-shore-road-market, continue to provide livelihood support for decommissioned fishermen by groups, and consolidate the social foundation of the Great Protection of the Yangtze River with open and transparent information and responsive public participation.
