# Strengthening community mental health competence in resource limited settings through a participatory multilevel framework

**출처**: Discover Mental Health
**발행일**: 2026-10-09

## 초록

There is growing interest in community-based mental health interventions in resource-limited settings, driven by the need for sustainable, locally grounded approaches. Yet many programmes remain dominated by individual and biomedical models and can overlook local knowledge, social networks, group identities, participation, and the material conditions that shape mental health. Community mental health competence offers a way to conceptualize communities as active partners able to understand, support, and act on mental health challenges. This paper synthesizes a mixed-method study conducted in Jamestown and Usshertown in Accra, Ghana. The study comprised a community survey (n = 384) and qualitative inquiry with 77 participants using individual/key-informant interviews, focus groups, and situated conversations. Findings were integrated with the social psychology of participation and community mental health competence traditions to derive five interconnected competence domains: representational, relational, identity, participatory, and structural competence. The framework was derived by mapping recurrent empirical mechanisms to distinct but interacting levels of community capability. Representational competence captures how mental health is understood and legitimized; relational competence concerns trust, social capital, and supportive ties; identity competence addresses the enabling and exclusionary effects of group membership; participatory competence concerns the conversion of shared concern and critical consciousness into collective action; and structural competence concerns the capacity to build alliances, secure resources, and act on wider institutional and socioeconomic determinants. A worked example demonstrates how the framework can guide intervention design from community diagnosis through co-production, implementation, and policy linkage. The framework provides an empirically grounded, culturally responsive roadmap for community mental health research and practice in resource-limited settings.
