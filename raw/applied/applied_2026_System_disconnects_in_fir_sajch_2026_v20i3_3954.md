# System disconnects in first food environments: Mothers’ lived experiences in two vulnerable urban communities, Western Cape Province, South Africa

## 초록

Background. The context in which mothers and caregivers make decisions about feeding during the critical first 1 000 days plays a crucial role in optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF). Objectives. To describe mothers’ lived experiences of IYCF and the system disconnects affecting first food environments. Methods. In this qualitative phenomenological study, mothers aged ≥18 years with children <5 years who were receiving a child support grant were purposively sampled from two vulnerable communities in the Western Cape Province, South Africa: Zweletemba (n=15) and Masiphumelele (n=17). In-depth interviews were conducted by mobile phone between February and April 2021, audio-recorded with permission, transcribed and thematically analysed. Results. Mothers who breastfed described breastfeeding as affordable and enjoyable and reported that it was preferred by their babies. Reasons for suboptimal practices included perceived insufficient breastmilk supply, the baby appearing hungry or refusing the breast, returning to or seeking work and fathers purchasing commercial milk formula (CMF) rather than contributing to the child’s general support. The high cost of CMF, particularly in the context of income pressures during the COVID‑19 lockdown, together with mothers spending more time at home, encouraged breastfeeding. IYCF advice in healthcare settings focused on promoting breastfeeding rather than providing detailed guidance on how to breastfeed or prepare CMF feeds safely. Specific brands of baby food, often encountered through advertising, were viewed as aspirational because of their convenience, palatability and cost. These included ultra-processed products, such as cereals or porridges, and ‘snacks’, including two-minute noodles. Conclusions. This study highlights the urgent need for health, food, education, urban, social and economic systems to align in support of improved IYCF within child-centred first food environments, thereby ensuring access to nutritious, safe and affordable diets and essential services that support children’s healthy growth and development.
