## 초록

Structural change and recurrent crises are causing major disruptions to societies and citizens. There-fore, both policy-makers and scientists widely encourage strengthening individuals’ resilience. Butsocial science resilience-literature is often criticized for ambivalent conceptualization and limitedpolicy-applicability. We therefore aim at identifying the determinants of individuals’ resilience whileaddressing these shortcomings. Our theoretical framework conceptualizes resilience in terms of empirically testable life-course capitals that help individuals maintain desired outcomes by meetingchanged resource-needs and reduced resource-access during disturbances. These life-course capitalsinclude not only an individual’s economic and human capital, but also their social capital (frommeso-level social networks) and “institutional capital” (support from macro-level public institutions).As such these capitals are multi-dimensional, multi-level, and interacting. Our empirical analysis employs multi-level regression models using longitudinal individual-level (n=16,071) and country-leveldata across 23 European countries during the COVID-19 pandemic. We find all life-course capitalvariables – lower economic arrears, savings, housing insecurity, education, health, social inclusion,public social expenditure, and government effectiveness – are positively associated with individual resilience. Social inclusion matters most, followed by health. We also find substitution-effects betweenlife-course capitals. Life-course capitals explain a total of 22% of the resilience-variance – explain-ing around 45% of the between-country(-group) variance (1-2% of the total variance), 36% of thebetween-individual variance (62%), and practically no within-individual resilience-variance (37%).Associations are rather stable across country-groups, but in Southern Europe social inclusion, education, and institutional capital matter slightly more. These findings suggest that a multi-dimensionalpolicy approach – prioritizing social inclusion and public health while considering country-specificconditions – can be promising.
