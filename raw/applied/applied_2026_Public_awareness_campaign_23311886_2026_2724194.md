# Public awareness campaigns and water conservation behaviour: extending the protection motivation theory

**저자**: Belinda Chiedza Senooane; Nombulelo Dilotsotlhe; Njabulo Happy-Boy Mkhize
**출처**: Cogent Social Sciences, Vol.12 No.1
**발행일**: 2026-10-03
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1080/23311886.2026.2724194
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Water scarcity has become one of the greatest environmental challenges of our time. However, despite increased pressure on households to conserve water, behavioural change remains inconsistent. This study examines how public awareness conservation campaigns promote water conservation by influencing consumers’ threat and coping appraisals and their attitudes towards water conservation. Specifically, the study extends the protection motivation theory (PMT) to incorporate campaign information usefulness and attitudes towards water conservation, and by assessing how water conservation communication campaigns shape the perceived risk of water scarcity and consumers’ perceived ability to engage in water conservation. Consumers were presented with a small sample of visual nudges from the Water Wise water conservation campaign and then asked to complete a self-administered questionnaire. PLS(SEM) was used to analyse data from 498 households in South Africa. The results demonstrate that the usefulness of campaign information significantly affects perceived severity, perceived vulnerability, response efficacy, and self-efficacy. Furthermore, perceived severity, self-efficacy, and response efficacy positively influence attitudes towards water conservation. The findings also confirmed the role of attitudes towards water conservation in driving intention to engage in water conservation and ultimately, water conservation behaviour. The study offers valuable new insights into water conservation, providing practical and theoretical guidance on how well-designed informational campaigns can enhance household water conservation efforts and strengthen behavioural responses.
