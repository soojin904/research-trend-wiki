# The problem of early school leaving—socioeconomic causes and potential solutions: a systematic literature review

## 초록

Early School Leaving (ESL), defined within the European Union policy space as individuals aged 18–24 who leave the education system without attaining upper secondary qualifications, affects approximately 3.1 million young people annually across EU. The repercussions of ESL are profound, such as diminishing tax revenues, increasing reliance on social services, and perpetuating cycles of poverty and poor health. Through a systematic literature review of 80 peer-reviewed papers published between 2003 and 2023, the purpose of this paper is to examine the socioeconomic causes of ESL. These causes include factors such as parental level of education and occupation, poverty leading to educational disadvantage, and lack of educational motivation. They also include factors related to schooling, such as school segregation, unsuitable curricula, teacher attitudes and expectations, and inflexible pathways through education and training. The findings reveal the complex interplay between socioeconomic status and educational outcomes. The discussion, while highlighting the interaction of social determinants contributing to underachievement and ESL, also outlines policy measures and interventions designed to counteract ESL's impact, emphasizing a joined-up approach to tackle these challenges. The paper concludes with specific recommendations for policymakers and practitioners aimed at reducing ESL and enhancing educational equity across Europe and beyond.
