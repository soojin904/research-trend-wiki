# Documenting the in-between: migrant-centered, multi-scalar epistemologies in irregular migration research

**저자**: Natalie Chwalisz
**출처**: Frontiers in Political Science, Vol.8
**발행일**: 2026-10-08
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpos.2026.1805152
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Research on irregular migration too often misses the journey itself. Studies of prospective migrants capture aspiration but not experience. Studies of successful arrivals reconstruct journeys filtered through relief and settlement. Neither sample position reaches the phases of transit and return where migration governance is most intensely felt and least visible in policy-centered analysis. This article argues that studying returnees, defined here as migrants who attempted irregular journeys and came back, whether through deportation, voluntary return, or failed crossing, opens an epistemic site that origin- and destination-centered designs cannot access. Drawing on 32 semi-structured interviews with Gambian returnees conducted through a locally embedded partnership, combined with over 1,000 regional transit surveys collected by the Mixed Migration Centre across West African transit hubs, the article demonstrates what a returnee-centered, multi-scalar design makes visible. From this vantage point, migration governance appears not as coherent policy but as a lived sequence of encounters with fragmented authority, negotiated intermediaries, and enforced waiting. These are conditions actively produced through border externalization rather than incidental to it. The article details three interlocking methodological choices that enable this perspective: sampling returnees as epistemic actors rather than policy subjects; pairing retrospective narration with distributional survey data to trace how individual decisions emerge within structural patterns; and building field access through locally embedded Gambian partnerships rather than international organizations, which shapes not only who can be reached but what they are willing to say. Each choice responds to a specific blind spot in conventional migration research designs. Together they offer a transferable framework for studying governance in corridors where affected populations are mobile, criminalized, and structurally hard to reach, and where the methods used to study them are never neutral.
