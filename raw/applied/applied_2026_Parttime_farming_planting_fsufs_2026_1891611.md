# Part-time farming, planting decisions and farmland operational performance

**발행일**: 2026-10-02

## 초록

Part-time farming is common in rural China, which may affect agricultural production, especially sustainable land use. Based on survey data of 507 farming households in Xinjiang, this study examines the impact of part-time farming on farmland operational performance and its internal transmission mechanism. Under the framework of rational smallholder theory and farmer behavior theory, this study constructs a unified analytical framework of part-time farming, planting decisions and farmland operational performance. The results show that farmers’ part-time farming has a significantly negative impact on farmland operational performance, and there is a nonlinear relationship between farmland area and operational performance. To address the potential self-selection bias arising from the non-random distribution of part-time farming behavior among households, this study adopts the Propensity Score Matching (PSM) method to conduct counterfactual analysis. Counterfactual results show that baseline regression overestimates the negative impact due to self-selection bias, and the negative net effect of part-time farming remains robust after balancing observed household endowments via propensity score matching. Mechanism analysis indicates a significant mediating role of the proportion of grain crops at the 5% level, with only weak marginal mediating evidence for the proportion of machinery input at the 10% level. The research findings provide important insights for improving the performance of agricultural land management and promoting sustainable agricultural development.
