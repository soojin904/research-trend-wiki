# Empirical Analysis of Burnout, Institutional Support, and Turnover Intention Among Nurses in Ghana: Urban Perspectives

**저자**: James Abadektemi Abasimi; Albert Tchey Agbenyegah
**출처**: International Journal of Business Ecosystem and Strategy (2687-2293), Vol.8 No.4, pp.181-192
**발행일**: 2026-10-09
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.36096/ijbes.v8i4.1198
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Concerns about nurse attrition are mounting. Despite this, limited empirical evidence exists on how different dimensions of burnout and institutional support interact in this context. This study investigates how burnout influences nurses’ turnover intention (TI) in urban Ghana and examines whether institutional support mitigates this relationship. A cross-sectional survey design was employed to collect data from 245 nurses across five Christian-based hospitals. Primary data is analysed and aided by the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) in addition to conducting direct evaluation to assess the moderating influences. The findings revealed that emotional exhaustion has a significant impact on TI, whereas depersonalisation has no direct effect. Institutional support also significantly reduces TI. In addition, emotional support strongly moderates the link between emotional exhaustion and TI, while access to resources partially moderates the effect of depersonalisation. The study shows that the negative effects of burnout on TI can be reduced through strong institutional support systems. The outcomes reinforce the applicability of Job Demands-Resources and Social Exchange theories and recommend more investment in both tangible resources and emotional support mechanisms to enhance nurse retention in high-demand urban healthcare ecosystems.
