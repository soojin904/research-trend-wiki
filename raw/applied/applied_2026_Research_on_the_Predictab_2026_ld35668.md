## 초록

The precise demand forecast in the entertainment industry remains a long-term challenge for content producers and distributors. Although big data technology has significantly enhanced the operational efficiency of the content distribution process, the inherent boundaries of demand predictability in the entertainment sector have not been fully studied. This article systematically sorts out the main obstacles to entertainment demand forecasting and evaluates the application scope and limitations of big data technology in this field. The research shows that although real-time tracking, recommendation algorithms, and A/B testing technologies have made considerable progress, big data still cannot solve three major problems: predicting bestsellers before going online, the phenomenon of delayed popularity, and the black box nature of word-of-mouth promotion mechanisms. This paper distinguishes Knight uncertainty from depth uncertainty from the perspectives of economics and statistics, and explains that a large amount of data does not necessarily mean predictability. Finally, a method for redefining the prediction target is proposed. This study provides a theoretical understanding of the deficiencies existing in predictive analysis and also offers a practical basis for strategic decision-making in the cultural and creative industries.
