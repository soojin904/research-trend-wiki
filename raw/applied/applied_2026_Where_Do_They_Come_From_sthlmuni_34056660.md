# Where Do They Come From and Where Do They Go? Reception and Retention of Immigrants in Swedish Municipalities

## 초록

Europe has seen large-scale refugee migration in the early 21st century, with Sweden receiving among the highest per-capita flows. While consequences of international migration have received attention, implications for internal migration flows remain underexplored. Researchers have neglected the combined regional consequences of initial refugee placement and subsequent internal migration. Swedish placement policy directs asylum seekers to municipalities with available housing, predominantly rural or post-industrial areas with weak labour markets and limited co-ethnic networks. This policy-driven placement contrasts with migration theory, which identifies economic opportunities and social networks as primary settlement determinants. Using longitudinal individual-level register data for 2003–2018, we construct dual origin and destination perspectives to track international migrants from initial placement through subsequent internal moves. Refugee policy concentrates migration into sparsely populated areas, generating onwards migration and a strong negative relationship between international in-migration and net population retention at the municipal level. This pattern varies by migrant origin group: Middle Eastern, North African, and Sub-Saharan African migrants are disproportionately subject to policy-driven placement, while Nordic, EU, and rest-of-world migrants follow free-choice settlement dynamics. International migration has become a large contributor to net internal migration flows across Swedish municipalities.
