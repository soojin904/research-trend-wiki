# Analyzing the Dual Social Consequences of Social Networks in Afghanistan

## 초록

This study investigates the impact of L1 interference on the English language learning of Afghan EFL learners, with a primary focus on grammatical errors and broader language learning factors. The objective is to identify common grammatical, phonological, and lexical difficulties influenced by Dari and Pashto, and to examine non-learning factors affecting learners’ performance. While teaching Afghan EFL learners, I observed that L1 interference from Dari and Pashto often caused grammatical errors that hindered their English proficiency, motivating this study to investigate its impact on language learning. A mixed-methods approach was employed, including semi-structured interviews and a two-part questionnaire administered to four purposively selected learners. Quantitative analysis measured the frequency of errors, while qualitative analysis explored patterns of L1 interference in spoken English. The findings show that Afghan EFL learners frequently produce subject–verb agreement, article, and preposition errors, alongside pronunciation and lexical challenges, which collectively impact their overall language learning. These results provide practical insights for EFL teachers to address L1 interference and improve learners’ English proficiency in classroom settings. Additionally, these findings are significant for EFL instruction in Afghanistan, as they provide practical guidance for addressing L1-induced grammatical and language learning challenges in classroom settings.
