## 초록

Social capital is often described as the invisible infrastructure that enables cooperation within societies. This paper examines the concept of social capital through the interconnected roles of trust, social networks, institutions, and collective action. It argues that trust alone is neither inherently beneficial nor sufficient for social development; rather, its impact depends on openness, accountability, justice, and institutional quality. The paper explores the different forms of social capital—bonding, bridging, and linking—and analyzes their relationships with economic development, entrepreneurship, public health, democracy, inequality, digital transformation, and artificial intelligence. It further argues that the strongest societies are not those with the highest levels of interpersonal trust, but those that successfully institutionalize trustworthy systems that enable fair cooperation among strangers. By integrating economic, sociological, institutional, and technological perspectives, the paper presents social capital as a multidimensional societal capacity essential for sustainable development and resilient institutions.
