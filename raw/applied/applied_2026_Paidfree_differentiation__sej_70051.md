# Paid‐free differentiation in freemium: Understanding the balance between discovery and cannibalization and its implications for paid product sales

**저자**: Joey van Angeren; Ksenia S. Podoynitsyna; Fred Langerak
**출처**: Strategic Entrepreneurship Journal
**발행일**: 2026-10-02
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1002/sej.70051
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Abstract Research Summary Entrepreneurs in digital markets often introduce free versions of their paid products to boost their visibility. This freemium strategy involves a trade‐off between discovery and cannibalization. We study how feature‐based differentiation between the paid product and its free version shapes this trade‐off and its consequences for paid product sales. We theorize that the relationship between paid‐free differentiation and paid product sales is inverted U‐shaped, because moderate levels of paid‐differentiation significantly reduce cannibalization costs while discovery benefits remain largely intact. We further argue that paid‐free differentiation is more salient when the value of the free version is enhanced through network effects, but less so if the free version contains value‐eroding, third‐party advertising. We analyze paid apps from the iOS App Store utilizing the freemium strategy. Managerial Summary Freemium strategies by which entrepreneurs introduce a free product version to boost the discoverability of their paid product have become enormously popular in digital markets. But aside from providing discovery benefits, the free version also cannibalizes the paid product's sales. We investigate whether this trade‐off can be resolved through value‐adding, feature‐based differentiation between the paid product and the free version. Analyzing paid apps from the iOS App Store utilizing the freemium strategy, we find that there is a point of optimal paid‐free differentiation that favors substantial, though not maximal, differentiation. Paid‐free differentiation is especially important when the free version provides more value to consumers by means of network effects. Paid‐free differentiation is less important when the free version contains third‐party advertisements that erode its value.
