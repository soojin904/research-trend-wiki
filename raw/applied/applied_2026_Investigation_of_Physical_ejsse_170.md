# Investigation of Physical Activity Levels and Psychological Hardiness Among Disaster Volunteers: A Cross-Sectional Study

**저자**: Nuh Furkan Yorulmaz; Hasan Erdem Mumcu
**출처**: Avrasya Spor Bilimleri ve Eğitim Dergisi, Vol.8 No.3, pp.450-462
**발행일**: 2026-09-27
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.47778/ejsse.170
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

This cross-sectional study aimed to examine physical activity levels and psychological hardiness among disaster volunteers and to investigate their associations with demographic, social, and lifestyle characteristics. The study included 330 disaster volunteers in Türkiye. Data were collected using the International Physical Activity Questionnaire–Short Form (IPAQ-SF), the Psychological Hardiness Scale (PHS), and a demographic information form. Independent-samples t-tests, one-way analysis of variance, Pearson correlation analysis, and multiple linear regression analysis were performed. Physical activity levels differed significantly according to disaster education received by family members or close acquaintances, whereas no significant differences were observed according to marital status, age, educational level, or daily sleep duration. Psychological hardiness differed significantly according to marital status, disaster education within the family or close social circle, age, educational level, and daily sleep duration. Physical activity showed a weak and statistically non-significant correlation with psychological hardiness (r = .089, p > .05). However, multiple regression analysis indicated that physical activity was a significant positive predictor of psychological hardiness (β = .104, p = .048), while age (β = −.208, p < .001) and sleep duration (β = −.224, p < .001) were significant negative predictors; gender was not a significant predictor. Overall, the findings indicate that physical activity and psychological hardiness represent complementary but largely distinct dimensions of disaster preparedness. Integrated preparedness programs addressing physical conditioning, psychological resilience, sleep and recovery, and social disaster education may contribute to more effective and sustainable disaster volunteer performance.
