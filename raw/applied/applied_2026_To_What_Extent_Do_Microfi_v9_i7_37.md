# To What Extent Do Microfinance Programs Improve the Entrepreneurial Success of Rural Women? Evidence from Credit Access, Financial Literacy, Training, And Mentorship Across Socio-Economic and Cultural Contexts

## 초록

Microfinance initiatives have long been advocated as tools to boost the entrepreneurial capacity of women in rural economies; mostly through better access to credit. However, there is now increasing evidence that credit alone may not be sufficient to guarantee the success of entrepreneurial activity among women in rural Africa, especially in the context of structural constraints, socio-economic and cultural factors that predominantly affect women. This paper focuses on the degree of impact that microfinance programs, including access to credit, financial literacy, training and mentorship, can exert on the success rate of entrepreneurship among rural women, as well as the changes in impact that may happen depending on the socio-economic and cultural context of a community. Using a secondary research methodology, the study synthesizes evidence from peer-reviewed academic literature, systematic reviews, and reports by international development institutions such as the World Bank, the International Finance Corporation, CGAP and IFAD. The multiple dimensions of entrepreneurial success are also examined in the analysis, such as entrepreneurship creation, survival of the business, increase in income, and autonomy in decision making. Findings from studies on the topic show that even though access to microcredit is associated with greater rates of business initiation, the effects on longer-term performance and profitability are greatly mediated by financial literacy, business training, and mentorship support (Armendáriz &amp; Morduch, 2010; IFC, 2014). Furthermore, the impacts of microfinance interventions also vary significantly by region depending on variations in education levels, household dynamics, institutional quality, as well as dominant gender norms (Sharma, 2018; IFAD, 2014). By considering both financial and non-financial mechanisms in one single analytical framework, this paper is a contribution to the literature on gendered entrepreneurship, focusing on the contextual conditions under which microfinance programmes are likely to result in significant and sustainable entrepreneurial outcomes for rural women.
