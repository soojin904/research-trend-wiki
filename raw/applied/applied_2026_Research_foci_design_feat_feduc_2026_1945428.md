# Research foci, design features, and accessibility challenges in digital mathematics and geography textbooks: a scoping review

**저자**: Maxim Brnic; Stefania Torri; Vanessa Macchia; Gilbert Greefrath
**출처**: Frontiers in Education, Vol.11
**발행일**: 2026-10-08
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1945428
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Introduction Digital textbooks are becoming increasingly important in mathematics and geography lessons, as interactive and multimedia features that go beyond the capabilities of printed resources are believed to offer particular learning benefits. Despite this growing significance, there has so far been no systematic research review that examines digital textbooks as a subject in their own right in these disciplines. The issue of accessibility, in particular, has been largely overlooked in previous textbook research and has gained particular relevance due to current legislation. This scoping review therefore aims to map the existing literature and to answer three questions: what are the main research foci, which design features of digital textbooks are being investigated, and in what way and to what extent is accessibility addressed. Methods Based on a systematic search in Web of Science and Scopus following the PRISMA-ScR framework, 78 studies (73 in mathematics, five in geography) were included in the final analysis corpus and evaluated using a common coding guide. Results The results reveal a young, rapidly growing, yet unevenly distributed field of research. Studies in the field of mathematics substantially outnumber those in geography, and the research focus lies predominantly on the development, production and use of digital textbooks, whilst effectiveness and content analyses are comparatively rare. At the feature level, research concentrates primarily on immediately visible functions such as text or visualizations, whilst system-based and adaptive functions are scarcely investigated. Although accessibility is clearly present in the literature, it is conceptualized inconsistently. Discussion The review highlights key research gaps, in particular the disciplinary asymmetry between subjects and the need for an explicit examination of accessibility, thereby providing a basis for future research and the inclusive design of digital textbooks.
