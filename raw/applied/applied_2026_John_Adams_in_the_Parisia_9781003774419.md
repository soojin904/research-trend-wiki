## 초록

While the diplomatic career of John Adams in Europe is frequently side-lined as a series of petty personal conflicts, the early transatlantic relationship was defined by a much more sophisticated intellectual struggle for sovereignty. John Adams in the Parisian Crucible reframes early American diplomacy by exploring how the ideological and cultural landscapes of the French Enlightenment directly shaped the foundations of American geopolitical power. This study moves beyond traditional diplomatic narratives to examine how high-level political theory collided with the brutal, pragmatic realities of international statecraft. Challenging the long-standing myth of Adams as an incompetent diplomat, this book reveals that his diplomatic conflict was the result of a calculated character assassination orchestrated by the Comte de Vergennes. The book’s distinctive feature is its innovative methodology that combines social network analysis with an intellectual history of Adams’s personal French library. By mapping these asymmetrical power dynamics, the book uncovers a republican realism forged in Paris that ultimately prepared Adams for the structural pressures of the US Presidency. This book is written for scholars, postgraduate researchers, and advanced students of early American history, the Age of Revolutions, and the history of international relations. It also holds strong appeal for biographers and general readers interested in a revisionist, politically sharp look at John Adams’s intellectual development and the true mechanics of the Franco-American alliance.
