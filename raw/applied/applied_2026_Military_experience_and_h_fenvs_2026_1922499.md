# Military experience and household clean cooking energy transition: evidence from China

## 초록

Introduction Household adoption of clean cooking energy (CCE) is critical for reducing household air pollution and advancing sustainable development. While previous studies have primarily emphasized economic, demographic, and policy determinants of CCE adoption, limited attention has been paid to whether early-life institutional experiences exert lasting influences on household energy choices. Drawing on imprinting theory and the life course perspective, this study examines whether household heads’ military experience (ME) is associated with subsequent household CCE adoption. Methods Using six waves (2012–2022) of panel data from the China Family Panel Studies (CFPS), this study investigates the association between household heads’ ME and household CCE adoption. A series of robustness, instrumental-variable, mechanism, and heterogeneity analyses are conducted to assess the stability of the findings and explore potential pathways and boundary conditions. Results The results show that household heads’ ME significantly increases the likelihood of household CCE adoption. Mechanism analyses provide evidence consistent with three potential channels: enhanced non-cognitive abilities, higher household income, and greater social capital. Heterogeneity analyses indicate that the association is more statistically robust among households with higher environmental awareness, rural household registration, and those located in coal-poor provinces. Additional analyses suggest that the imprinting effect is stronger among individuals with longer military service but gradually weakens as the time since discharge increases. Conclusion The findings suggest that military experience may have persistent implications for household environmental behavior beyond the period of service. By integrating the life course perspective into imprinting theory, this study extends understanding of how early institutional experiences can shape later household energy choices and provides implications for promoting China’s household clean energy transition.
