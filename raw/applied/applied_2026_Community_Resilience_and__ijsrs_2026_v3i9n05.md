# Community Resilience and Educational Continuity in Conflict-Affected Areas: Lessons from Mamfe Municipality, Southwest Cameroon

**저자**: Gilbert Zechia Mofor; Nadine Temgoua Yemelong; Ebot Honrine Ashu

## 초록

Over the past decades, armed conflicts have consistently dismantled educational institutions, especially in conflict-affected areas in the world. These areas, have showcased varied remarkable resilience by developing capacities that sustain educational continuity. The Mamfe Sub-Division in Cameroon haven undergone similar scenario is staggering a retrieval phase against educational disruption through community-led strategies resilience. This study examines the state of pre-and during crisis educational landscape and institutional profiles of Mamfe, and how community resilience mechanisms have partially recovered and sustain educational continuity in the midst of the socio-political conflict in the study area. Using a mixed-methods approach and a stratified random sampling, the study administered 128 structured questionnaires, and in-depth interviews with 10 resource persons, across 4 targeted Secondary Educational communities in Mamfe, notably Enowchunge Memorial College, Besongabang; Presbyterian High School, Besongabang; Government High, Mamfe; and Government Bilingual High School Mamfe. Findings revealed appalling disruption, with 45.3% of school infrastructure rated poor, classroom resources found inadequate with 64.8%, a 70.3% decline in enrolment rate, and 78.1% reporting a net negative educational impact (χ² = 116.26, df = 2, p < 0.001). Despite these setbacks, the study identified three main resilience mechanisms including multi-stakeholder dialogue that has enabled partial educational continuity, adaptive pedagogy during periods of resource scarcity, and community mobilisation for school reopening. Owing to this, infrastructure reconstruction was 70.3%, security enhancement 66.4%, and psychosocial support 54.7% emerged as priorities recovery pathways which were observed as priority interventions. The study also advocates for crucial multi-stakeholder collaboration among government, NGOs, and community actors to effectively restore educational infrastructure, stabilise schools functioning, integrate trauma-informed, and crisis-responsive pedagogical approaches into the secondary and high school’s curriculum.
