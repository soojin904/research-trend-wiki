# The Contribution of Community Involvement to the Quality of Education in Secondary Schools

**저자**: Gemma Gillo; Georgina Mugisha; Patrick Ategwu Ogar
**출처**: Journal of Teaching Learning & Curriculum, Vol.2 No.2, pp.594-614
**발행일**: 2026-10-01
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.61227/pgvprb58
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

This study examined the contribution of community involvement to the quality of education in secondary schools in Mpwapwa District, Dodoma Region, Tanzania. The study was guided by Social Capital Theory, which emphasizes the importance of trust, social networks, shared norms, and cooperation in improving educational outcomes. A mixed-methods research approach employing an explanatory research design was used. Study participants involved 99 students who were obtained through simple random sampling, 52 teachers through stratified sampling, 10 parents through snoeball and purposive sampling was used to obtain, 6 heads of schools, 6 Ward Education Officers, and 1 District Education Officer making a total of 174 participants. Data were collected using likert scale questionnaires, interviews, and focus group discussions. Quantitative data were analyzed using SPSS Version 26 through descriptive statistics, while qualitative data were analyzed thematically. The reliability of the questionnaire was established through Cronbach’s Alpha, which yielded a coefficient of 0.776 for 20 items, indicating acceptable internal consistency. The findings revealed that community members participated substantially in school activities, with 94.9% of students confirming their participation. Community involvement was particularly evident in monitoring students’ academic progress, attending school meetings and events, motivating students to study, supporting school infrastructure, promoting discipline, and enhancing school accountability and transparency. However, the findings also showed limited community assistance with homework and school projects, largely due to parents’ limited educational backgrounds and socioeconomic constraints. Furthermore, community involvement did not always translate directly into improved academic performance, suggesting that its effectiveness depends on the nature, quality, and structure of participation as well as other factors such as teaching quality and availability of learning resources. The study concludes that meaningful school-community partnerships are important for improving educational quality when participation is structured, purposeful, and supported by effective school leadership. The study recommends strengthening school-community communication, establishing structured community participation mechanisms, providing parent education programmes, promoting community-supported academic assistance, and strengthening accountability structures to enhance the contribution of community involvement to quality education.
