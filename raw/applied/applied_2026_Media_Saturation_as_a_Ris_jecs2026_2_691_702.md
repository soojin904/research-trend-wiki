# Media Saturation as a Risk Factor for Anxiety in Adolescents: Theoretical Foundations and Implications for Education

## 초록

Thesis. The present study is grounded in the thesis that media saturation in contemporary society constitutes a key risk factor for rising anxiety among adolescents, and that an adequate understanding of this dynamic presupposes an interdisciplinary theoretical framework necessary for the systematic delineation of possible responses and preventive strategies. Concept. The authors analytically examine the correlation between media saturation and adolescent anxiety through three complementary theoretical perspectives: sociological-cultural (Turkle, Bauman), empirical-psychological (Haidt, Twenge), and existential-spiritual, in which the philosophical anthropology of Søren Kierkegaard occupies a distinctive place. Attention is given to the role of the family and the school as potential protective environments. The study draws on an analytical synthesis of relevant international literature and research findings from the Central European context, with particular focus on Slovakia and the Czech Republic. Results and Conclusion. The authors demonstrate that media saturation does not function as an isolated causal factor of anxiety but enters a complex network of mutually conditioned psychosocial dynamics, including the weakening of critical thinking (Šeboková et al., 2024), the disruption of emotional and social intelligence, and the erosion of the sense of meaning in the absence of authentic community. The family and the school possess an irreplaceable protective potential whose development must be systematically supported within the framework of educational sciences. Cognitive Value. The study offers an original interdisciplinary synthesis at the intersection of educational sciences, developmental psychology, and philosophical anthropology, enriched by the Kierkegaardian existential dimension, which remains insufficiently reflected in the domestic academic discourse in this context.
