# POST-TRAUMATIC STRESS DISORDER INTERVENTIONS FOR CHILDREN AND ADOLESCENTS AFFECTED BY WAR IN LOW- AND MIDDLE-INCOME COUNTRIES IN THE MIDDLE EAST: A NARRATIVE REVIEW

## 초록

Background: War-related post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) represents a significant public health burden for children and adolescents in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) across the Middle East. Despite protracted conflicts in Syria, Iraq, Yemen, and Palestine, the evidence base for effective interventions remains fragmented. This review aim to synthesize the literature on psychosocial and psychotherapeutic interventions for war-related PTSD in children and adolescents in Middle Eastern LMICs. Methods: A narrative literature review with systematic search principles was conducted across PsycINFO, PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and Arabic-language indices. Studies published in English or Arabic between 2000 and 2026 that evaluated psychosocial or psychotherapeutic interventions for war-related PTSD in children and adolescents (≤18 years) in Middle Eastern LMIC settings were included. Findings were synthesised thematically by intervention modality. Results: Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT) and Narrative Exposure Therapy (NET) demonstrate the strongest evidence for PTSD symptom reduction, with effect sizes varying by fidelity and setting. School-based, group-based, and family-based interventions show promise but are limited by methodological weaknesses. Cultural adaptations—incorporating local idioms of distress, religious coping metaphors, community engagement, and participatory design—are essential for acceptability. Implementation is constrained by severe workforce shortages, financial instability, pervasive stigma, ongoing conflict, and ethical complexities around informed consent and re-traumatisation. The evidence base is characterised by small sample sizes, high attrition, heterogeneous outcome measures, and publication bias. Conclusion: Scalable, ethically sound, and culturally integrated interventions delivered through strengthened health and education systems are urgently needed. Future research must prioritise rigorous randomised designs, long-term follow-up, effectiveness-implementation hybrid trials, and culturally validated outcome measures to inform policy and practice in conflict-affected settings.
