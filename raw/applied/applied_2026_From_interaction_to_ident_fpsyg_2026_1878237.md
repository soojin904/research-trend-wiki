# From interaction to identification: a mixed methods study on the formation mechanism of group identity among Ultimate Frisbee club members

**저자**: Ruyin Chen; Fangjin Chang; Kaijuan Xu
**출처**: Frontiers in Psychology, Vol.17
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1878237
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Background Lifestyle sports like Ultimate Frisbee have emerged as important settings for social connection amid urban social atomization. Nevertheless, the voluntary and non-contractual nature of these clubs means they continue to face persistent challenges in sustaining membership. Although prior research has explored group identity in various sporting contexts, the mechanisms through which ritualized interaction and social exchange jointly contribute to members' identification remain insufficiently understood. Objective This study investigates the formation mechanism of group identity among Ultimate Frisbee club members by integrating Interaction Ritual Chain theory and Social Exchange theory, and empirically tests whether emotional energy and relational benefits serve as parallel mediating pathways. Methods A sequential exploratory mixed-methods design was employed. Study 1 adopted a grounded theory approach, using semi-structured interviews with 22 club members across multiple Chinese cities to inductively identify core antecedents and construct a theoretical model. Study 2 employed a cross-sectional survey design, with structural equation modeling applied to a sample of 332 Ultimate Frisbee club members to quantitatively test the hypothesized pathways. Results The grounded theory analysis indicated a dual-pathway mechanism encompassing an affective pathway (interaction rituals → emotional energy → group identity) and a rational pathway (interaction rituals → relational benefits → group identity). The SEM results indicated that both emotional energy and relational benefits significantly mediated the relationship between interaction rituals and group identity. The mediating effect of relational benefits—encompassing trust and social acceptance—was larger (33.58% of total effect) than that of emotional energy (14.97%). Conclusion These findings integrate Interaction Ritual Chain theory and Social Exchange theory into a unified framework, suggesting that rational evaluations of social reciprocity were more strongly associated with identity formation than transient emotional experiences in non-contractual sports clubs. The results point to the central role of relational calculus in voluntary organizations where formal incentives are absent. From a practical standpoint, club managers may consider prioritizing relational rewards and the development of ritualized interaction settings to enhance long-term member retention.
