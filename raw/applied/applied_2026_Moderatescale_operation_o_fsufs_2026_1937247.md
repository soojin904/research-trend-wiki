# Moderate-scale operation of family farms in China: underlying logic, practical constraints, and optimization pathways for sustainable food systems

## 초록

The transformation of sustainable food systems must reconcile production efficiency and supply resilience with the protection of smallholder livelihoods, and for economies with large populations and limited arable land this reconciliation rests heavily on how the scale of family-farm operation is understood and organized. The academic community has long held theoretical disagreements on this issue between static efficiency standards and dynamic equilibrium perspectives, and no integrated explanatory framework that combines economic logic, social context and temporal dimensions has yet been established. Drawing on new institutional economics, economic sociology, family life-cycle theory and intergenerational transmission theory, and proceeding through theoretical deduction, institutional context analysis and secondary evidence from policy documents, official statistics and literature, this paper develops a triple-constraint dynamic equilibrium framework for the moderate-scale operation of family farms in China; its purpose is explanatory rather than estimative, since no uniform optimal scale is defensible across heterogeneous regional endowments, crop systems, service conditions and family structures. The framework holds that moderate scale is not a fixed value set externally but a dynamic equilibrium interval of operating scale: the economic-efficiency constraint sets its lower bound, the social-embeddedness constraint sets its upper bound, and the intergenerational-sustainability constraint operates as an independent temporal condition that also moderates the position and width of the interval as the family life cycle and succession arrangements change. Seen thus, the factor-market, organisational-coordination and temporal-dimension failures observable in practice appear as three structural tensions that jointly compress the feasible interval and constrain the contribution of family farms to sustainable food-system goals. The contribution lies in bringing social embeddedness and intergenerational sustainability into the analysis of moderate scale, moving beyond a single-efficiency account and offering an integrated explanatory framework for the evolution of family-farm scale in transition economies with fragmented landholdings and household-centred production. The framework applies directly to family farms and other family-centred operations; its three constraints are analytically general, whereas their relative binding strength, the interval's position and the associated policy instruments remain conditional on the institutional environment, particularly China's collective land ownership and household contract responsibility system, so that its reference value for other transition economies is conditional rather than universal.
