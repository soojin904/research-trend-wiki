## 초록

This chapter examines the role of ethnic and social networks in shaping business opportunities in the Grand Duchy of Finland (1809–1917), focusing on the dynamics of the Finnish cotton industry. It traces the evolution of Finnish business life, from its mercantile origins to its industrialization, within the context of the Russian Empire. Despite legal barriers that excluded migrant groups, such as Orthodox, Jewish, and Islamic communities, from full economic participation under Old Swedish legislation, these ethnic minorities played a crucial role in transforming the nineteenth century Finnish economy, the cotton industry being a prime example. By analyzing Finland’s status as an imperial borderland, the chapter explores how migrant groups navigated ethnic, national, and religious boundaries, and how their business networks evolved in response to these shifting dynamics. The chapter applies theories of ethnicity, legal status, and cultural identity to understand the role of extended family groups in Finland’s diversification and internationalization. It offers a perspective on Finnish economic history within the broader Russian imperial framework and addresses gaps in scholarship concerning the contributions of these sometime-overlooked migrant communities.
