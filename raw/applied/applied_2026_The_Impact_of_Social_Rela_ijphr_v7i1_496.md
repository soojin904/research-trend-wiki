# The Impact of Social Relationships on Mental Health and Emotional Well-Being among Young Adults

## 초록

Social relationships play an essential role in shaping mental health and emotional well-being among young adults, particularly during developmental transitions involving personal independence, educational responsibilities, employment challenges, and changing interpersonal expectations. This study aims to explore how social relationships contribute to young adults' experiences of psychological well-being, emotional support, loneliness, and interpersonal challenges. A qualitative phenomenological approach is proposed to examine the subjective meanings individuals assign to their relationships with family members, friends, romantic partners, and broader social networks. Data collection involves semi-structured interviews, with thematic analysis guiding the interpretation of participants' experiences. The literature-informed analysis identifies four interconnected dimensions: emotional support within social relationships, interpersonal experiences associated with mental health, social connectedness and loneliness, and coping strategies for maintaining healthy relationships. Supportive interpersonal connections may provide emotional reassurance, psychological security, and opportunities for developing resilience, whereas interpersonal conflicts, emotional invalidation, and perceived rejection may contribute to psychological distress. Meaningful social connectedness depends primarily on relationship quality, mutual trust, and emotional responsiveness rather than the frequency of social interactions. Digital communication further influences emotional experiences by facilitating interpersonal contact while introducing challenges associated with social comparison and relational expectations. Constructive communication, emotional regulation, personal boundaries, and support-seeking represent important strategies for navigating interpersonal difficulties. This study provides a conceptual foundation for understanding the relational dimensions of psychological well-being and highlights the importance of relationship-centered approaches to mental health promotion among young adults across diverse social environments and developmental circumstances in contemporary society.
