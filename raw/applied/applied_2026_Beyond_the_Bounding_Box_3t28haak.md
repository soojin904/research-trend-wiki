# Beyond the Bounding Box

## 초록

This article argues that advances in computer vision models and digitization within the past few years have changed the landscape of cultural heritage data. Tasks that previously required manual annotation, fine-tuning, and substantial computational resources can now be executed with significantly greater expediency using machine learning. Moreover, tasks that were previously thought to be impossible are now increasingly feasible. My work builds on earlier scholarship that extracted data from business directories using methods such as manual annotation that produced the Text Encoding Initiative (TEI), early OCR technologies, and the fine-tuning of computer vision models by leveraging the capabilities of transformer-based models to extract the entities within these directories. In evaluating multiple approaches, I demonstrate that transformer-based methods can achieve high reliability in producing structured datasets from historical tabular sources. I present a method for future scholars to produce structured datasets from nineteenth-century lists. Consequently, these methods allow for future scholars to analyze the data contained within these directories in ways that were previously inaccessible or impractical such as large-scale data analysis, pattern recognition, and cross-referencing. At the same time, this article argues that these methods open a speculative horizon. They help us to locate traces of what we do not know we do not know, especially in relation to marginalized or deliberately minimized archives (Johnson; Taylor). In this context, digitized and extracted business directories are no longer static historical artifacts but rather become dynamic objects that have been remediated through a digital lens. In turn, this article argues that technical advances allow a remediation of business directories beyond inert archival remnants into dynamic objects capable of supporting new historical and humanistic inquiry. As a demonstration, I release a subset of African American titles extracted from Rowell’s and Ayer’s directories, illustrating how computational remediation can amplify overlooked archives.
