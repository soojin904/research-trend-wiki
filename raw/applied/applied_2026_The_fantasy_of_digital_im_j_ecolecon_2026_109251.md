# The fantasy of digital immateriality: AI, ecologically unequal exchange, and appropriation of lived human time

## 초록

The purported immateriality of AI technologies is as illusory as the general understanding of conventional technologies as simply the application of politically neutral knowledge to nature. The materiality of technologies and their foundation in global inequalities has been illuminated by studies of ecologically unequal exchange (EUE). The aim of this article is to extend the applicability of EUE theory to digital technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI) by highlighting how even the data that they process have an inevitably biophysical history. AI technologies represent a novelty mainly in the sense that, in addition to exploiting extractive areas through EUE, they are based on the appropriation of data from the public. We argue for understanding the materiality of data as derived from lived human time. AI's dependence on data can thus be regarded as representing and embodying biophysical processes and asymmetric transfers implicating issues of justice.
