# How Socio-Cultural Structures Shape Strategies of Climate-Resilient Social Protection Pathways in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania

**저자**: Salua Haji Othman; Tatu Mtwangi Limbumba; Wilbard Kombe
**출처**: Journal of arts, humanities and social science., Vol.3 No.3, pp.127-136
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.69739/jahss.v3i3.2301
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Low-income households in Sub-Saharan Africa have limited resilience to climate change shocks despite the region's unique socio-cultural support structures. This article explores how socio-cultural structures such as gender norms, social networks, social cohesion, trust, indigenous knowledge, customs and traditions, and cultural values of reciprocity shape climate-resilient social protection pathways among low-income households. Drawing on the climate-resilience development pathway (CRDP) and social capital theoretical frameworks, this study uses a qualitative, exploratory case study design. Using a sample of 100 households, 6 key informants, and 2 focus group discussions, the findings show that integrating socio-cultural structures with climate-resilient initiatives such as social protection is a pathway to inclusive and sustainable resilience to flood shocks among low-income households in urban informal settlements such as Dar es Salaam. Findings also revealed that, even when social protection initiatives are integrated with climate-resilient features, socio-cultural structures remained relevant and effective for climate-resilient pathways. Socio-cultural structures informed the design of climate-resilience (mitigation and adaptation) strategies and built climate-resilient social protection pathways, including short- and long-term resilience such as anticipatory, absorptive, adaptive, and transformative resilience. This study empirically validates CRDP and offers a context-sensitive understanding of socio-cultural structures as mediators, demonstrating their importance in designing and implementing climate-resilient, inclusive, accessible, and sustainable social protection initiatives. The study contributes to policy by framing socio-cultural structures as enabling systems that shape resilience strategies and pathways.
