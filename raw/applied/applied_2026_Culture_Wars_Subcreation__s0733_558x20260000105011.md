## 초록

Cultivated meat – meat grown from animal cells in bioreactors – has become a symbolic flashpoint in contemporary culture wars, framed not as a technical innovation but as either moral salvation or civilizational threat. We argue that these conflicts arise through temporalized narrative subcreation: the construction of emotionally charged storyworlds that embed cultural values within divergent orientations toward past and future, continuity and change. Analyzing proponent and opponent discourse, we show how both sides mobilize the same core values – tradition, identity, and autonomy – yet transform them into polarized narratives by projecting them into distinct temporal storyworld genres. Our framework theorizes culture wars as contests of temporal meaning-making, where legitimacy hinges on competing subcreated worlds that render innovations morally legible. By revealing the temporal and narrative structures underpinning these conflicts, we offer a portable analytic lens for understanding legitimacy struggles surrounding other disruptive innovations, including climate action, reproductive technologies, and AI governance.
