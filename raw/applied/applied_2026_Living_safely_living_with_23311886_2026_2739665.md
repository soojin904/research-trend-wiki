# Living safely, living within limits: gurus’ perspectives on well-being in transgender communities in Pakistan

**저자**: Sadia Malik; Tahir M. Nisar
**출처**: Cogent Social Sciences, Vol.12 No.1
**발행일**: 2026-10-03
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1080/23311886.2026.2739665
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Transgender people in Pakistan are largely subject to stigma, violence, institutional non-attendance, economic exclusion, and a lack of institutional support. This study examines senior Gurus’ perceptions and practices regarding Chelas’ well-being in transgender communities. The study uses an interpretive qualitative design in which 15 senior Gurus from urban and semi-urban areas of Pakistan were interviewed using a semi-structured format. The interviews were translated and transcribed into English, and analyzed using the Gioia methodology. The results show that Gurus view their governance as a response to external threats, such as harassment, violence, exploitation, and economic insecurity. The freedom to move, work, and socialize was often described as "care” in ways that increased safety and community, a common theme. The results highlight a core paradox in the Guru–Chela relationship: seeking safety and belonging can come with limits on individual freedom. Gurus also explained how such practices became institutionalized and passed on from generation to generation as previous Chelas took up leadership positions. This study indicates that the wellbeing of marginalized communities is shaped by both internal and external aspects of their social lives, particularly day-to-day social arrangements and relationships within their communities.
