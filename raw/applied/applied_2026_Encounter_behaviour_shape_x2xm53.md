# Encounter behaviour shapes ecological distributions

## 초록

Ecology’s foundational models keep only the first moment of the stochastic distributions of encounters. Yet, encounter behaviour (how animals search, aggregate, hide and move) sets the whole distributional shape: zeros, variance, skew and, where particular behaviours build them, heavy tails. That shape decides vital rates, persistence and spatial pattern, because the rates involved are threshold-like and read the tail, not the mean. I argue that departures from the well-mixed Poisson are the rule; that identifiable, measurable behaviours generate them; that where persistence rests on a refuge, a threshold or a self-reinforcing assembly, removing them removes persistence; and that the task ahead is to derive the shape forward from measured behaviours.
