## 초록

Purpose: This study examines how emerging FinTech-driven credit scoring models can promote inclusive lending by reducing structural biases embedded in traditional financial assessment systems. It focuses on the role of machine learning and alternative data in facilitating fairer access to credit for underserved populations across various regions. Need for the study: Conventional credit scoring mechanisms often exclude individuals with limited formal financial histories, reinforcing inequality in access to financial services. With the rapid expansion of digital footprints, there is a pressing need to understand whether data-driven FinTech solutions can address these gaps in an ethical and effective manner while supporting sustainable finance goals. Methodology: The research employs a qualitative case study approach, informed by insights from cognitive neuroscience, to investigate how technology-assisted decision systems mitigate common human biases in financial judgement. Empirical analysis is centred on Mama Money’s credit assessment practices in South Africa, with comparative reflections drawn from Southeast Asia and Latin America. Findings: The study finds that integrating alternative data sources – such as mobile usage, social networks, and digital payment behaviour – significantly enhances credit risk evaluation and inclusivity. However, challenges related to data privacy, algorithmic fairness, and fragmented regulatory frameworks persist. Practical implications: The findings offer actionable insights for financial institutions and policymakers seeking to align credit scoring practices with ethical standards and sustainable finance objectives. Establishing clear regulatory guidelines is essential to ensure fairness, trust, and scalability across global markets.
