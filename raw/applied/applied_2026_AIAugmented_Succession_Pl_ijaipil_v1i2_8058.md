## 초록

Volunteer organizations play a critical role in civil society, yet many face increasing difficulty finding, developing, and replacing volunteer leaders. Demographic change, evolving patterns of civic participation, competing demands on volunteers’ time, and volunteer fatigue create significant challenges for leadership continuity. Although succession planning has been extensively studied in corporate and professional contexts, less attention has been given to volunteer organizations, where authority, engagement, and leadership development operate differently.This article examines succession challenges in volunteer organizations and argues that succession planning should be understood as an ongoing responsibility of governance and stewardship rather than a response to leadership vacancies. Drawing on literature concerning succession planning, nonprofit leadership, volunteer management, social capital, and civic engagement, the article proposes an actionable six-step model emphasizing leadership commitment, role clarity, succession-risk assessment, identification and development of potential successors, and redesign of leadership roles to strengthen continuity and leadership pipelines. A hypothetical community arts organization illustrates the model’s practical application.The article further examines how artificial intelligence can augment succession planning in resource-constrained volunteer organizations through knowledge capture, role clarification, leadership development, and continuity planning. AI is conceptualized as a capacity-building tool rather than a substitute for human judgment. Volunteer leaders remain responsible for organizational context, confidentiality, fairness, governance, and succession decisions. The resulting approach positions AI-augmented succession planning as a human-centered strategy for strengthening leadership continuity, institutional knowledge, and organizational resilience.
