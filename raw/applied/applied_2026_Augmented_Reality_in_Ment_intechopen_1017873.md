## 초록

Augmented reality (AR) is an immersive technology similar to virtual reality (VR). While VR places humans in a simulated virtual environment, AR brings digital objects into the real world for human interaction. Its safe and controlled therapeutic use ensures the reliability of the intervention and fosters treatment adherence. Therapists define requirements – incorporating ethical and emotional considerations – for software developers to create AR programs. It is applicable in situations where the real-world context of the disorder is unsafe, costly, or logistically complex – such as the fear of flying, which makes *in vivo* exposure difficult – by substituting the real scenario with a simulated computational environment. Evidence points to an expansion in the use of AR for assessing and treating individuals with mental disorders, though the specific characteristics of each disorder must be taken into account. The technology utilizes biometric and behavioral data – such as facial expressions and audio, among others – which require safeguards to ensure patients' or users' privacy; exposure of such data could lead to psychological distress and treatment dropout. Factors such as costs, specialized expertise, robust infrastructure, high-quality internet connectivity, spatial and reality simulators, and user familiarity can act as both limitations and challenges in advancing the use of this technology to achieve better outcomes.
