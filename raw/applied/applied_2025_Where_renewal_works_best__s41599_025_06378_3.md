# Where renewal works best? Unrevealing spatiotemporal effects of community renewal via DID and RF analysis

**저자**: Hong Ni; Jinliu Chen; Pengcheng Li
**출처**: Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, Vol.13 No.1
**발행일**: 2025-12-10
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-06378-3
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Community renewal has emerged as a pivotal strategy to mitigate spatial obsolescence and address the multifaceted challenges of rapid urbanization. However, its effects on urban spatial vitality remain spatially heterogeneous across different development stages, and the social factors shaping residential life satisfaction during renewal are still in dispute. To address these dual challenges, this study employs a socio-spatial theoretical framework and develops a two-stage analytical model. First, utilizing multi-source big and small data (e.g., geotagged social media data, geospatial POIs, questionnaire survey data), we implement a Difference-in-Differences (DID) analysis to quantify the causal effects of community renewal on urban vitality across spatiotemporal dimensions. Specifically, central districts exhibit statistically significant vitality enhancements: Gusu (DID: 0.063, p < 0.001), SIP (DID: 0.054, p < 0.01), and SND (DID: 0.052, p < 0.01), along with Suzhou overall (DID: 0.054, p < 0.001). Conversely, Wuzhong and Xiangcheng, as peripheral regions, demonstrate negative effects. Second, combining Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) and Random Forest (RF) models to identify key determinants of resident satisfaction. Green space coverage (β = 0.123, RF = 30) and educational facility density (β = 0.315, RF = 41.5) exhibit the strongest positive correlation with satisfaction. These findings highlight the necessity of fostering community cohesion through localized socio-spatial integration by bridging ex-post impact evaluation with actionable socio-spatial strategies. The study advances urban renewal theory by demonstrating how spatial interventions can catalyze social integration, while providing policymakers with evidence-based guidelines for optimizing renewal efficiency in heterogeneous urban contexts.
