## 초록

Poland’s 2004 accession is treated as a structural shock that may have strengthened resilience to later crises. The chapter compares Poland, a new Member State, with Germany, an established EU economy, using quarterly Eurostat data, Vector Autoregression models and Impulse Response Functions. GDP reactions to export shocks are assessed during accession, the global financial crisis, the sovereign-debt crisis and COVID-19. Poland displays stronger and more persistent responses relative to its economic size, while Germany shows earlier but smaller relative movements and greater exposure to large adverse shocks in its export mix. Market access, capital inflows, institutional convergence, EU funds and trade diversification appear to have improved Poland’s adaptive capacity. The chapter portrays enlargement as a resilience-building mechanism rather than as a one-off integration event.
