# The dark side of marketing deception: a multidimensional analysis of customer punitive intentions and switching behaviour

**저자**: Manal A. Ghoneim; Wageeh A. Nafei; Abdallah M. Elshaer; Abu Elnasr E. Sobaih; Ali Saleh Alshebami; Ahmed E. Abu Elnasr
**출처**: Frontiers in Communication, Vol.11

**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

The purpose of this study is to examine the effect of marketing deception (MD) on customer punitive intentions (CPI) in the hospitality industry, with customer switching intentions (CSI) serving as a mediating mechanism. Marketing deception is conceptualized as a multidimensional construct comprising product, pricing, distribution, and promotion deception. Data were collected from customers in Egypt who had used Facebook to search for, compare, book, reserve, or purchase hospitality services, including hotel accommodation, restaurants, travel, and tourism-related services. A total of 415 questionnaires were distributed, of which 350 valid responses were retained for analysis. The findings indicate that marketing deception has a significant positive effect on customer punitive intentions and customer switching intentions. Customer switching intentions also significantly increase customer punitive intentions and partially mediate the relationship between marketing deception and customer punitive intentions. These findings suggest that deceptive marketing practices not only encourage customers to disengage from hospitality service providers but also increase their likelihood of engaging in punitive behaviors. The study extends the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) framework by identifying customer switching intentions as the psychological mechanism through which marketing deception translates into punitive behavioral responses, while providing practical implications for hospitality managers seeking to strengthen customer trust and reduce the adverse consequences of deceptive marketing practices.
