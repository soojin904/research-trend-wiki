## 초록

This chapter highlights the importance of friendship and social networks in Jakarta’s toolkit for urban survival. It traces the appearance of friendship debt, characterized as informal personal debt, in practically all social relations as a result of Jakarta’s financial pressures. The chapter draws on classical anthropological ideas of kinship, friendship, and reciprocity to analyze how and why friendship debt becomes a mechanism for redistribution in Jakarta. Taking the point of view of the lender as ethically and socially obligated to comply with a borrower’s request, the chapter shows how the terms of reciprocity in urban friendships are never fully fair or reciprocal.
