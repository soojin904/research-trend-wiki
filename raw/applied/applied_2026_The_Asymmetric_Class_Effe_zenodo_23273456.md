## 초록

Contemporary educational reform in democratic societies is increasingly characterized by egalitarian mandates aimed at reducing academic friction: the elimination of objective standardized testing, the weakening of competitive tracking, and the systematic lowering of curricular difficulty. While publicly championed as triumphs of social justice and inclusive pedagogy, this paper demonstrates that such levelling-down policies produce severely asymmetric class effects. Utilizing Bourdieu’s theory of cultural capital and Lasch’s critique of elite revolt, we conceptualize this phenomenon as 'Voluntary Ladder Dismantling'—a process wherein the mass electorate actively strips public education of academic rigor, inadvertently destroying the sole institutional mechanism capable of facilitating upward mobility for talented working-class youth. Consequently, as objective academic metrics dissolve, selection processes default to qualitative markers, social networks, and private educational investments exclusively accessible to affluent dynasties. Far from eroding class hierarchy, egalitarian educational reforms provide an ideal moral alibi for the consolidation of a neo-hereditary patriciate.
