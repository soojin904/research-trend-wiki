# Association between parental support from immigrant parents and anxiety/depression of second-generation teenagers

**저자**: Ellice Lim; Talia Dardis; Natalia M. Rojas; Sungwoo Lim; Bonnie D. Kerker
**출처**: Frontiers in Developmental Psychology, Vol.4
**발행일**: 2026-07-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fdpys.2026.1861151
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Introduction Anxiety and depression are growing concerns for teenagers from immigrant families. Parental support may help reduce teenagers' mental health burdens, but evidence among immigrant families is limited. The purpose of this study was to (1) test the hypothesis that parental support is associated with teenagers' mental health in Asian and Latino immigrant families, and (2) assess if this relationship differs by race/ethnicity, gender, age, and parents' education. Methods Surveys were administered among a convenience sample of 101 Asian and Latino teenagers in Westchester County and New York City, New York. Outcomes were depressive and anxiety symptoms, measured by the modified PHQ-9 and GAD-7 for teenagers, respectively. Emotional and informational support from parents were measured by the Perception of Parental Support questions. The association between parental support and teenagers' mental health was assessed using linear regression after accounting for age, gender, parents' education, and household income. Interactions between parental support and potential moderators were also tested. Results About 15% of teenagers reported moderate or moderately severe depression, while 11% reported moderate or severe anxiety. Both emotional and informational support were negatively associated with teenagers' depression and anxiety ( p &amp;lt; 0.01). However, informational support was not associated with mental health among Asian teenagers. The relationship did not significantly differ by gender, age, and parents' education. Discussion Higher support from immigrant parents was associated with lower scores of teenagers' depression and anxiety. Moderation by race/ethnicity points to the importance of tailored approaches when developing interventions to promote teenagers' mental health among immigrant households.
