# ‘An intimate glimpse of the societies – hollywood, high, café’: access, participation and cinema celebrity in Jean Howard’s candid film star photography

## 초록

This article examines the career and work of overlooked celebrity photographer Jean Howard, during the 1940s and 1950s, when Hollywood’s studio system and its stars were at the height of their influence. Her photographs of stars at Los Angeles nightspots and exclusive Beverly Hills social events, published in magazines including Life, Look, Vogue, Collier’s, and Harper’s Bazaar, offered audiences and later historians a distinctive view of Hollywood’s social elite, raising questions of access, intimacy, visibility, and social distinction. Drawing on interviews, magazine features, and archival materials from the University of Wyoming’s American Heritage Centre, the article explores Howard’s creative process and professional aims, focusing on how she negotiated intervention, mediation, and representation in her work and relationships with stars. It argues that her photography bridged two distinct eras in the visual culture of film stardom and celebrity photography.
