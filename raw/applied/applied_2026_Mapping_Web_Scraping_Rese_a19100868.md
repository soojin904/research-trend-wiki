# Mapping Web Scraping Research for Competitive Advantage: A Hybrid Bibliometric and Unsupervised Machine Learning Approach Using LDA Topic Modeling

**저자**: MERVE VURAL ALLAHAM
**출처**: Algorithms, Vol.19 No.10, pp.868-868
**발행일**: 2026-10-10
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3390/a19100868
**수집 키워드**: text analysis

## 초록

This study examines the structural, semantic, and temporal evolution of strategy- and competition-oriented web scraping research, focusing on the legal and governance boundaries of data-driven competitive advantage. A purposively delimited corpus of 389 full-text articles indexed in the Web of Science and published between 2002 and 2025 was analyzed using bibliometric techniques and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), an unsupervised machine learning method for probabilistic topic modeling. Bibliometric analysis identified publication trends, keyword relationships, co-citation structures, and the visible conceptual organization of the field, while full-text LDA revealed ten latent themes. The findings show that data extraction, web crawling, social media content, and algorithmic structures remain central. However, the literature increasingly addresses market dynamics, consumer insights, organizational capabilities, digital skills, geospatial analysis, and data-driven decision-making. Comparing keyword clusters with full-text topics indicates thematic correspondence across several core areas, while demonstrating that full-text analysis captures contextual and application-oriented themes less visible in author keywords. Legal, ethical, and governance concerns constitute a distinct but relatively peripheral stream and show selective and uneven integration with strategically oriented themes, with stronger connections to data-extraction and social-media contexts than to market- and consumer-oriented themes. The study contributes methodologically by integrating full-text topic modeling with bibliometric network analysis and conceptually by identifying legitimate access, purpose of use, and organizational governance as boundary conditions of strategic value.
