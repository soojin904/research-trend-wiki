# Climate Change Risks Disclosure and Cost of Capital of Listed Oil And Gas Firms in Nigeria

**저자**: Emmanuel Emah Silas
**출처**: IIARD INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL OF GEOGRAPHY AND ENVIRONMENTAL MANAGEMENT, pp.106-106
**발행일**: 2026-09-28

## 초록

The pervasiveness of climate change in recent times cannot be ignored due to its effect on the activities of businesses. Consequently, the disclosure of the impact of climate change risks on the business is expected to douse the anxiety that investors are exposed to. This study was therefore conducted to investigate the effect of climate change risk disclosure on cost of capital of listed oil and gas firms in Nigeria. The independent variable being climate risk disclosure, was proxied by flood control cost disclosure, waste management cost disclosure, greenhouse gas emissions control cost disclosure, disaster control cost disclosure and environment restoration cost disclosure; while the dependent variable being cost of capital was measured by weighted average cost of capital (WACC). The study adopted an ex-post facto research design and utilized a panel data of ninety (90) pooled observations gathered from nine (9) listed oil and gas firms in Nigeria over ten (10)-year period of 2014 to 2023. The data were analysed with descriptive statistics and panel multiple regression technique using E-views version 10.0 statistical package. The results of analysis revealed that flood control cost disclosure has a significant negative relationship with cost of capital; waste management cost disclosure has a significant positive relationship with cost of capital; greenhouse gas emissions control cost disclosure has a non-significant positive relationship with cost of capital; disaster control cost disclosure has a significant negative relationship with cost of capital, and environment restoration cost disclosure has a non-significant positive relationship with cost of capital, of listed oil and gas firms in Nigeria. Based on the findings, the study concludes that climate change risk disclosure significantly influences the cost of capital of oil and gas firms in Nigeria. The study recommends, amongst others, that oil and gas firms should prioritise the disclosure of important factors affecting the firms such as climate change.
