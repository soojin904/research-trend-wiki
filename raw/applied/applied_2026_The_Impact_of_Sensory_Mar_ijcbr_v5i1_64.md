# The Impact of Sensory Marketing on Emotional Attention and Visitor Satisfaction of Gen Z in Heritage Tourism Places in Sri Lanka

**저자**: Charith Prabuddha; D. K. T. Dangalla
**출처**: International Journal of Contemporary Business Research, Vol.5 No.1, pp.80-96
**발행일**: 2026-10-12

## 초록

It is often assumed that visiting a historical site is purely about what you see, but this paper tries to investigate this domain further. It looks at how Sensory Marketing functions in practice, specifically for Generation Z tourists exploring Sri Lanka’s heritage landscape. The core question wasn't just whether "good senses" equal "happy tourists." the study aimed to understand the mechanics of it. So, the study looks at the link between sensory inputs and Visitor Satisfaction, but it also considers a potential mediator as, Emotional Attention. This represents the psychological process where sensory stimuli transcend background noise to elicit an emotional response. To get a real sense of this, data were collected from 384 Gen Z travellers who had recently walked through key Sri Lankan heritage sites. To ensure empirical rigor, the analysis was conducted using PLS-SEM in SmartPLS 4 to test the relationships. The results were pretty interesting, though perhaps not entirely shocking. It turns out that sensory cues do a lot of heavy lifting. They significantly boost both emotional attention and overall satisfaction. But here is where the nuance comes in. The analysis suggests that Emotional Attention acts as a partial mediator. Thus, sensory marketing is effective partly because it forces the visitor to pay emotional heed to their surroundings. It serves as a psychological bridge. For site managers, the implication is distinct: reliance on visual history is insufficient. To ensure satisfaction for younger travellers, it is necessary to curate the entire atmosphere, the sounds, the textures and the vibe to trigger that emotional engagement.
