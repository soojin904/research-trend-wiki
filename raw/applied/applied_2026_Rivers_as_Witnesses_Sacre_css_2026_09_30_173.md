# Rivers as Witnesses: Sacred Geography, Historical Memory, and American Indian Sovereignty along the Missouri River

## 초록

This paper is about the Missouri River and the Oceti Sakowin: Dakota, Lakota, and Nakota peoples and others who lived along the river, especially the Mandan, Hidatsa, and Arikara.It examines spatial change in the Missouri river -treaties, dams and reservoirs, drowned communities, and continuing ceremonial significance -to make the case that it is a living historical document, reflecting contestation of land and water use and management regimes.Tribal lands were curtailed in the nineteenth century through treaties, and considerable bottomland was flooded in the twentieth century when dams were constructed under the Pick-Sloan Missouri Basin Program, resulting in the loss of cultural features.Water protection is connected to treaty rights and historical survival through oral history, tribal commemorations and cultural practices, and the Standing Rock movement against the Dakota Access Pipeline.It foregrounds and refocuses American Indian knowledge and sovereignty and reveals that environmental change is also political and cultural history.
