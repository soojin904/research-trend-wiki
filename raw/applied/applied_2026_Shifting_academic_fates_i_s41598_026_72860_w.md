# Shifting academic fates in early adolescence are marked by concurrent behavioural change rather than baseline profiles

**저자**: Chihiro Hosoda; Kenchi Hosokawa; Takuto Matsuhashi; Nanase Takahashi; Yu Hayashizaki; Yutaka Matsuzaki; Ryuta Kawashima
**출처**: Scientific Reports
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-72860-w
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Abstract Academic performance in childhood is highly stable in relative rank, yet the behavioural characteristics that accompany stable versus changing achievement standing remain unclear. We longitudinally tracked 16,946 students (grades 4–9; approximate grade-derived baseline ages 11.9 and 11.4 years) over 3–4 years in two contrasting Japanese cities, using achievement tests standardized annually within city and grade, and lifestyle–learning questionnaires. All trajectories therefore describe changes in students’ relative standing, not absolute learning growth. Most students’ relative standing was stable and classified well from baseline questionnaire responses, a distinction that largely reflects baseline achievement level, with stable high standing accompanied by consistent daily routines, family support, and active classroom engagement. Approximately 9% of students rose or fell substantially; these shifts were weakly classified from Year 1 behavioural profiles but were marked by concurrent changes in study investment, motivation-related responses, and digital-media use. Behavioural information available through Year 3 added minimal prospective information about Year 4 standing beyond prior achievement (ΔR² = 0.002), indicating that these changes accompany, rather than forecast, shifts in relative standing. Stable and changing academic standing were thus marked by distinct behavioural profiles—observable markers whose causal status and modifiability warrant prospective and experimental study.
