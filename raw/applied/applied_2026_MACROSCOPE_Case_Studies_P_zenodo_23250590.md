## 초록

MACROSCOPE — Case Studies Part Two is an extended 70-page research monograph examining the empirical foundations of the MACROSCOPE framework across biological, cognitive, social, and digital systems. Building on Case Studies Part One, which examined Operation Northwoods and the Visual Mandela Effect, this work investigates network organization, collective synchronization, informational memory, recursive feedback, and the persistence of information structures across successive carriers. The monograph analyzes independent experimental research involving human social networks, ant colonies, collective memory, laboratory microcultures, artificial-language transmission, and social learning in chimpanzees. Particular attention is given to the experimental persistence of group norms, linguistic structures, and learned behaviors after the original initiators or demonstrators are no longer present. The findings provide independent empirical corroboration of several constituent mechanisms described by MACROSCOPE, especially network-dependent collective behavior, retained informational influence, and finite transmission across changing carriers. The framework's integrated quantitative phase-memory dynamics remain a separate subject for prospective validation. The analysis follows the previously published MACROSCOPE parameter register without introducing new framework variables.
