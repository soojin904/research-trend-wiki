# Graph-based machine learning in urban form characterization: a critical methodological review with structured taxonomy and methodological framework

**저자**: Mansoor Ali; Abdulrazzaq Shaamala; Junyan Yang; Tan Yiğitcanlar
**출처**: Journal of Geographical Systems
**발행일**: 2026-09-26
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10109-026-00524-4
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Abstract Graph-based machine learning (graph-based ML) offers a structurally well-suited paradigm for encoding the relational properties of urban form, yet its application to urban morphological characterization remains fragmented across disciplinary boundaries. This study presents a critical methodological review of graph-based ML for intrinsic urban form characterization, synthesizing 24 studies through a transparent, reproducible search, screening, and extraction process. The review develops a four-axis taxonomy covering urban form elements, graph construction strategies, learning paradigms, and morphological applications, alongside a reproducible seven-stage methodological framework. Grounded in established principles of representation learning, the review advances the argument that upstream representational choices are a consequential and insufficiently examined source of variation in graph-based urban morphology, with the abstraction of cities into nodes and edges shaping which morphological patterns become computationally accessible. Across the 24 reviewed studies, within-study comparisons show that graph construction strategy and morphometric attribute selection can substantially alter analytical outcomes, a pattern recurring across unsupervised, supervised, and semi-supervised paradigms; the corpus does not, however, contain studies that vary representation and architecture systematically enough to establish which factor contributes more to outcome variation overall. The review further identifies graph construction strategy selection as following from three jointly operating determinants (analytical scale, theoretical conception of meaningful adjacency, and task type), a mechanism explaining representational diversity without reducing it to arbitrary methodological preference. A critical gap is identified: current construction strategies provide limited explicit encoding of spatial discontinuity — its causes, permeability, temporal status, and morphological meaning — constraining analysis of fragmented urban conditions. Three tractable future directions are proposed to address this limitation; these extend beyond the reviewed evidence rather than emerging from the corpus as findings of equivalent standing.
