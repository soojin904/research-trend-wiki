# The politics of forest biomass co-firing in Indonesia: Explaining policy implementation through the Actor–Network–Interest (ANI) framework

**저자**: Sukasno; Nunuk Supriyatno; Johanes P.G. Sutapa; Ahmad Maryudi
**출처**: Journal of Cleaner Production, Vol.578, pp.149583-149583
**발행일**: 2026-09-27
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2026.149583
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Biomass co-firing in coal-fired power plants has been promoted as a transitional strategy for increasing the share of renewable energy in Indonesia's electricity sector to support the national goal of net-zero emission. This paper analyses actor's behaviour to shed light on why the implementation of biomass co-firing policy has yet to reach the target level required to achieve national targets. To do so, this paper develops a novel Actor–Network–Interest (ANI) Framework that predicts actors' implementation behaviour based on the interaction between their structural positions within policy networks and the consistency of their formal and informal interests. It employs a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design, combining Social Network Analysis (SNA) of 65 actors with in-depth interviews and qualitative stakeholder analysis. SNA was used to identify influential actors through degree and betweenness centrality, while qualitative analysis explored the formal and informal interests underlying actors' policy responses. The results reveal a cross-sectoral network dominated by actors from the energy and forestry sectors, with limited participation from market and civil society organisations. Perhutani and PLN Energi Primer Indonesia (PLN EPI) occupy the most central positions due to their strategic roles in biomass supply and electricity system integration. The interaction between network position and interest finds three dominant implementation patterns: 1) selective–pragmatic, 2) administrative–selective, and 3) compliant–administrative. These findings support the explanatory and predictive value of the proposed framework. At the same time, brokerage functions remain weak, indicating limited capacity to coordinate interests across sectors. Consequently, biomass co-firing has been implemented largely as an incremental adjustment compatible with existing coal-based electricity systems rather than as a transformative energy transition strategy. The findings highlight the importance of stronger brokerage mechanisms, better-aligned incentives, and more effective cross-sectoral coordination to accelerate renewable energy transitions.
