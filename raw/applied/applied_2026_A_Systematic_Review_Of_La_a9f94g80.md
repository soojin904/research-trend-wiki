## 초록

Large language models (LLMs) have rapidly transformed natural language processing by enabling advanced capabilities in text understanding, generation, and reasoning across diverse domains. The growing body of research published between 2020 and 2025 reflects both significant methodological advances and expanding real-world adoption. This review presents a systematic and comparative analysis of recent LLM studies, integrating application-oriented research from IEEE venues with foundational and journal-level contributions from Springer, Elsevier, and ScienceDirect. The reviewed literature is categorized based on methodological approaches, application domains, and evaluation strategies to provide a structured synthesis of current trends. Comparative analysis reveals a clear distinction between applied deployments emphasizing task-specific performance and foundational studies focusing on scalability, alignment, interpretability, and ethical governance. The review further identifies critical research gaps related to evaluation consistency, computational efficiency, explainability, and responsible deployment. Open challenges such as hallucination, prompt sensitivity, and resource-intensive training are discussed alongside emerging solutions. Finally, future research directions are outlined, emphasizing integrated evaluation frameworks, sustainable model design, hybrid reasoning systems, and human-aligned development. By consolidating insights across heterogeneous research streams, this review aims to support the development of reliable, transparent, and socially responsible large language model systems.
