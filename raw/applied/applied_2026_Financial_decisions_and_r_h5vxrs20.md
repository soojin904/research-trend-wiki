# Financial decisions and retirement readiness among teachers of government-aided secondary schools in Sheema District. A cross-sectional study.

**저자**: Angella Atuhaire; Johnson
**출처**: SJ Education Research Africa, Vol.3 No.4, pp.14-14
**발행일**: 2026-10-01
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.51168/h5vxrs20
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Background The study aimed to establish the relationship between financial decisions and retirement readiness among teachers of government-aided secondary schools of Sheema District. Methodology A cross-sectional correlational mixed-methods study involved 206 teachers aged 45–59 years from nine government-aided secondary schools in Sheema District, selected using census sampling. Quantitative data were collected using structured five-point Likert questionnaires, while qualitative data were obtained through structured interviews with nine head teachers. Validity was assessed using CVI, and reliability using Cronbach’s alpha. Quantitative data were analysed using frequencies, percentages, Pearson correlation, and multiple regression in SPSS version 25, while qualitative data underwent content analysis. Results A total of 188 of 206 teachers responded (91.3%), while all nine head teachers were interviewed (100%). Males comprised 60.1% and females 39.9%; 57.4% taught arts and 42.6% sciences. Financial decisions showed mixed practices: 68.6% compared prices (M=3.71, SD=0.93), while 87.8% disagreed that they saved for retirement investment (M=1.71, SD=0.89), and 53.7% disagreed that they belonged to a health insurance scheme (M=1.54, SD=0.63). Retirement readiness was similarly mixed, with 60.1% reporting alternative income sources (M=3.36, SD=1.17), but 63.3% lacking six-month emergency funds (M=2.21, SD=1.02) and 66.5% reporting debt (M=2.15, SD=0.99). Qualitative themes included limited insurance and investment diversification, dependence on salary, debt and emergency-finance constraints, alternative income generation, retirement lifestyle preparation, and social adjustment. Financial decisions had a low positive significant relationship with retirement readiness (r=.326, p<.01), with investing (r=.295, p<.01) and insuring (r=.263, p<.01) also significant. Spending and borrowing showed no significant relationships with retirement readiness at the statistical level. Conclusion Financial skills contribute to retirement readiness, with a low positive relationship between financial skills and retirement readiness. Recommendation School administrators and education stakeholders should strengthen teachers’ investment and insurance financial decisions to improve retirement readiness.
