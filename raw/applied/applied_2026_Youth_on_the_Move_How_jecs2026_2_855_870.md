# Youth on the Move: How Education Shapes Labour Migration and Demographic Trends in Georgia

## 초록

Aim This study examines the demographic and economic consequences of labour emigration from Georgia during the post-Soviet transition, with emphasis on youth migration, remittance dynamics, and socio-cultural effects of sustained emigration. Methods The research combines contextual analysis of Georgia’s migration history with a sociological survey of student youth. It explored migration intentions, family migration history, adaptation abroad, and the socio-economic impact of remittances. Quantitative data were compiled into tables reflecting age, gender, destination preferences, education level, and remittance use, linking migration outcomes to broader societal transformations. Results Labour emigration has become a major demographic force, contributing to population decline, ageing, and regional imbalances. Remittances stabilize household economies: 31.2% are spent on food and clothing, and 24.6% on housing improvements. Youth show strong emigration potential, with Germany and the United States as preferred destinations. A notable mismatch exists between emigrants’ qualifications and employment abroad, often resulting in underemployment and entry into secondary labour markets. Despite positive adaptation experiences, return migration remains limited due to Georgia’s persistent economic problems, affecting both education and social cohesion. Conclusion Labour emigration has reshaped Georgia’s demographic landscape and survival strategies. While remittances provide short-term relief, long-term consequences include brain drain, social fragmentation, and structural labour market vulnerability. Addressing these challenges requires consistent migration policies, improved data systems, targeted support for return and reintegration, and investments in education to strengthen societal resilience.
