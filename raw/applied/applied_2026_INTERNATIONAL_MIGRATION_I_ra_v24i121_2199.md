## 초록

International migration is a social process in which conditions in the place of origin, opportunities in destination areas, and individual and household capabilities influence the decision to migrate. This article aims to analyze international migration from Honduras through the push-pull theory. Methodologically, a qualitative study based on a bibliographic and documentary review was conducted, drawing on research studies and official statistics. In Honduras, poverty, precarious employment, violence, food insecurity, and climate-related impacts drive migration, while wage differentials, employment opportunities, family reunification, security, and migrant networks shape destination choices. Their influence varies according to household resources, gender, age, territory, and border policies. The article concludes that the push-pull approach is useful when combined with perspectives on migration networks, aspirations and capabilities, human security, and forced mobility.
