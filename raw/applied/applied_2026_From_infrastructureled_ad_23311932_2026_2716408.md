# From infrastructure-led adaptation to circular climate governance: evidence from agricultural enterprises under the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor

**저자**: He Baocheng; Amir Jamil
**출처**: Cogent Food & Agriculture, Vol.12 No.1
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1080/23311932.2026.2716408
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Large-scale infrastructure investment is increasingly promoted as a climate change adaptation strategy in developing agrarian economies, yet whether such investments generate resilient outcomes remains contested. This study examines the role of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) in shaping climate change adaptation pathways for agricultural enterprises in Pakistan, emphasizing institutional and governance mechanisms. Circular climate governance refers to iterative institutional feedback systems where policy outcomes are monitored and fed back into governance processes to strengthen adaptive capacity, contrasting with linear infrastructure delivery. Drawing on institutional theory, human capital theory, and integrated water resource management (IWRM), adaptation capacity is conceptualized as a higher-order construct comprising institutional capacity, technical training, collaborative governance, community input, stakeholder engagement, and technical capabilities. Using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) on survey data from 325 stakeholders in Pakistan and China, the study tests six mediation pathways and one moderation hypothesis. Results show that CPEC investments do not directly enhance climate adaptation outcomes; rather, effects are mediated through institutional capacity, technical training, and technical capabilities. Collaborative governance, local participation, and transboundary water management show non-significant effects, indicating persistent governance failures and institutional lock-in. Findings demonstrate that infrastructure-led adaptation without circular institutional design fails to deliver sustainable resilience, while institutional capacity-building represents the most actionable pathway for CPEC-driven agricultural climate adaptation.
