# Left Off The Map: How Venture Capital’s Geographic Concentration Perpetuates Rural Economic Stagnation

## 초록

Venture capital has become a central engine of American innovation, yet its distribution is geographically concentrated, with the vast majority of funding flowing to a handful of Metropolitan hubs—Boston, New York, and the Bay Area. This paper examines how limited access to venture capital shapes a long-term economic trajectory of rural communities, arguing that the funding gap is not incidental but the product of a self-reinforcing feedback loop rooted in elite university networks, established industry clusters, and investor preference for geographic proximity. Drawing on federal and academic research on community development infrastructure capital, small business investment company (SBIC) programs, and regional economic data, this paper traces how the absence of institutional financial infrastructure in rural areas discourages entrepreneurship, accelerates “brain drain” among young talent, and locks local economies into a narrow set of low-growth industries such as agriculture and retail. This analysis also considers how the rise of AI-focused startups—which tend to generate fewer local jobs and traditional small businesses—may be widening the gap between urban and rural economic outcomes. In contrast, evidence from Community Development Venture Capital (CDVC) and SBIC-funded ventures suggests that even modest capital investment in rural areas can generate substantial employment growth and diversify local economies. Based on these findings, this paper recommends a full strategy for rural communities: cultivating community-based funding mechanisms such as crowdfunding, building durable local infrastructure including chambers of commerce, and expanding access to business lending to support entrepreneurship independent of traditional VC hubs
