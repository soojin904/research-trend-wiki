## 초록

Brands continually seek innovative ways to differentiate themselves, capture attention, and remain memorable. One such approach is bus branding, which transforms public buses into moving advertisements that reach large, diverse audiences across multiple locations. Unlike traditional billboards, television, radio, print, or online advertising, Out Of Home (OOH) advertising reaches consumers as they move through public and commercial spaces. Despite its widespread use and growing popularity, limited research has examined what makes bus branding effective, particularly in emerging markets such as Pakistan. Building on Information Processing Theory, this study examines how consumers process and recall advertising messages displayed on branded buses. It investigates the effects of attention, message clarity, interest, and mobility on advertising recall. Using online surveys, a total of 442 responses were collected from Karachi, Pakistan’s largest metropolitan city, where bus branding is quite visible. Using SmartPLS4, the study assessed the extent to which these factors contribute to consumers’ ability to recall advertising messages. The findings reveal that message clarity is the strongest driver of recall, followed by attention and mobility, suggesting that advertisements that attract notice from repeated exposure are more likely to be remembered. In contrast, interest was found to have a weaker effect, indicating that finding an advertisement interesting does not necessarily guarantee that its message will be retained in memory. This study contributes to the growing literature on out-of-home advertising by extending Information Processing Theory to the context of bus branding. It also offers practical insights for advertisers and media planners by demonstrating that effective bus branding requires more than visibility alone; advertisements must be attention-grabbing, easy to understand, and strategically placed to maximize exposure and recall.
