# Dance and Emulation: Exploring the Adoption of Role Models Among Nigerian Youths with David Jones’s Anti-Drug Abuse Music Intervention

**저자**: Ezekiel Onyebuchi Eze; Eliza Govender
**출처**: Communicare Journal for Communication Studies in Africa, Vol.45 No.1, pp.78-95
**발행일**: 2026-07-28

**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Abstract The prevalence of drug abuse continues to be one of the foremost public health issues globally, with Nigerian youth among the hardest-hit victims of this social problem. An anti-drug-abuse entertainment education intervention using music as a medium can offer new hope for vulnerable Nigerian youths. However, understanding the portrayal of positive role models in music and their impact on youth behaviour is crucial for effective entertainment education. This article employs reflexive thematic analysis of data from a study involving Nigerian youths from seven universities to address the question, “To what extent have David Jones’s music entertainment education characters influenced the adoption of role models among Nigerian youths exposed to the interventions?” Key findings reveal that Nigerian youths exposed to David Jones’s music adopted role models from the music characters, encouraging the emulation of positive behaviours. Nevertheless, despite the educational value of the entertainment education messages, undesirable conduct among music characters can deter listeners. This study suggests that when designing entertainment education for youths, it is essential to consider music characters that exhibit positive drug-use behaviours. In conclusion, entertainment education on anti-drug abuse, which targets youths, should incorporate music as a medium in the Nigerian context.
