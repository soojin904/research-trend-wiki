# Given and Chosen Ties: Status, Personal Networks, and Political Behaviour in Britain

## 초록

Political participation in Britain is stratified not only by where people stand but by who stands with them. Bringing social network analysis to the study of social stratification, this article draws on ego-networks from the British Household Panel Survey, which link respondents to their closest friends and to partners and relatives who are themselves panel members. It examines how the status of these networks relates to turnout, political interest, and party choice across four general elections, and whether given ties, such as parents and siblings, differ from chosen ties, such as partners and friends. Status homophily is strongest among friends. The status of partners, friends, and the family of origin is associated with each outcome. The family of origin is as closely associated as chosen ties, and more closely with interest, whereas adult children stand largely apart. Status also reaches further than behaviour: two steps from ego, alters' status is still associated with ego's turnout, while their turnout is not. These associations reflect stable differences between people, pointing to durable status milieux. Between 1992 and 2005, these milieux came to separate voters from abstainers more, and Labour from Conservative voters less.
