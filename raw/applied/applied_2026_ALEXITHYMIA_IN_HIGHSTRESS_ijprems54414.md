# ALEXITHYMIA IN HIGH-STRESS OCCUPATIONS: AN INTEGRATIVE RISK AND INTERVENTION MODEL FOR MILITARY, HEALTHCARE, AND LAW ENFORCEMENT PERSONNEL

## 초록

The military, medicine, and law enforcement, the highest of high-stress occupations, regularly have their members immersed in trauma exposure, moral injury, and long-term occupational stress.Such jobs are also generally associated with high-profile rates of psychosomatic and psychiatric phenomena and have been specifically linked to alexithymia, a subclinical trait involving impairment in emotional awareness and expression, externalizing orientation of cognition and fantasy.Alexithymia probably represents both a predisposition and possibly an occupational influence.Theoretical and empirical literature concerning alexithymia, occupational stress, and emotion regulation are reviewed in proposing an integrative risk model, the Occupational Alexithymia Vulnerability-Reinforcement (OAVR) Model.The model identifies two interrelated occupational pathways to alexithymia, a dispositional vulnerability pathway and an occupationally reinforced pathway, and specifies how alexithymia mediates and moderates the relationship between occupational stress exposure and downstream outcomes, including burnout, PTSD, substance use, and interpersonal difficulty.Building on this risk model, a complementary three-tier intervention model is proposed, integrating recruitment-stage screening, organizational culture and training reform, and clinical treatment approaches suited to alexithymic presentations (e.g., interoceptive and mentalization-based methods).Suggestions are presented concerning occupational screening, training, organizational reform, and clinical intervention, along with directions for further research.
