# Moving for Wages, Staying for Care: Rural-Urban Migration and Women's Economic Inequality in China

## 초록

There have been significant economic opportunities from rural-to-urban migration in China, although these opportunities are unevenly distributed in favor of men or women within households. This structured narrative review evaluates the issue of gender economic inequality in China through four interlinked aspects: wage employment, unpaid work, land and asset ownership, and social protection. In doing so, the paper compares migrant women who move to urban areas for wage employment with those staying behind in rural areas, taking them to be part of a translocal household strategy. From the reviewed literature, it becomes clear that migration allows women migrants to earn wages, expand their social networks, and even acquire bargaining power, but these are limited by hukou discrimination, occupational segregation, low social-insurance coverage, and child-care related employment interruption. Women left behind tend to engage in more farm work, childcare, eldercare, and domestic chores, but increased responsibilities do not lead to increased control over land, income, or decision-making. Thus, migration creates an empowerment vs. burden paradox where increased household income is achieved at the expense of increased workload and insecurity for women. There are wide variations among the outcomes for educational attainment, marital and parental status, regional development, land tenure security, access to public services, and information communication technology (ICT).
