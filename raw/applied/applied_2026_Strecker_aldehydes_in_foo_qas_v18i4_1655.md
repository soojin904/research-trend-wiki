# Strecker aldehydes in food systems: formation mechanisms, influencing factors, and impact on flavor attributes

## 초록

Strecker aldehydes are key aroma-active compounds formed via the degradation of amino acids in the presence of reactive carbonyl compounds, and are closely associated with Maillard reactions and lipid oxidation in food systems. Owing to their low odor thresholds and high reactivity, these compounds play a significant role in shaping the flavor profiles of thermally processed and fermented foods. This review provides a comprehensive overview of the formation mechanisms of Strecker aldehydes, highlighting the influence of amino acid precursors and α-dicarbonyl intermediates. Major groups of Strecker aldehydes, such as sulfur-containing, branched-chain, and aromatic compounds, are discussed in relation to their chemical structures and sensory characteristics. Their occurrence in various food matrices, such as starch-based products, bakery items, meat systems, and fermented foods, is examined to illustrate the impact of raw material composition and processing conditions on their formation. Key factors affecting generation of Strecker aldehyde, such as temperature, pH, water activity, amino acid type, and lipid oxidation, are evaluated critically. The dual role of these compounds in flavor attributes is emphasized, as they contribute to desirable aroma notes but may also lead to off-flavor if present in excess. Strategies for controlling their formation, such as thermal optimization, precursor management, antioxidant use, and packaging design, are also discussed. Overall, Strecker aldehydes are highlighted as essential contributors to development of flavor and important targets for quality control in modern food systems.
