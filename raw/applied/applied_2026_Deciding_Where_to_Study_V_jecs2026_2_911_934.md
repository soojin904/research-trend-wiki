# Deciding Where to Study: Validation of a Scale to Understand University Choice in Ecuador

**저자**: Nicolás Sumba-Nacipucha; Antonio Sánchez-Bayón
**출처**: Journal of Education Culture and Society, Vol.17 No.2, pp.911-934
**발행일**: 2026-09-26
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.15503/jecs2026.2.911.934
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Aim. The objective of the research is to develop and validate a scale that measures the factors influencing university choice in Ecuador, integrating approaches from neoclassical synthesis and behavioural economics, considering that this decision is mediated by perceptions, experiences and social pressures, beyond responding solely to a rational analysis of costs and benefits. Methods. The study employed a quantitative methodology. It began with a literature review that identified the most relevant factors addressed in previous research, and then constructed a proposed scale validated by expert judgement. The scale was applied to a sample of 633 students from private universities in Ecuador. Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) were used to validate the scale. Results. The resulting instrument is structured into five dimensions: neoclassical factors, social influences and prior knowledge, institutional reputation, individual factors, and social life, showing how economic reasoning and social, individual, and contextual conditions interact in students' decision-making. Conclusions. Although traditional factors such as educational offerings, infrastructure, location, and costs remain decisive, university decisions are also influenced by behavioural aspects such as social influences, institutional reputation, individual factors, and social life. The validation of the scale reinforces the idea that university applicants do not make decisions based solely on a strictly rational approach, which rules out the homo economicus view. Practical application. Insights from this study may guide universities that wish to strengthen their strategies for evaluating, positioning, and recruiting applicants.
