# Mediated civic engagement: online activity and community participation on the Indian subcontinent

**저자**: Christopher Etheridge; Nazra Izhar; Moses U. Okocha
**출처**: Frontiers in Communication, Vol.11
**발행일**: 2026-07-27
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1862977
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Introduction Digital technology has been increasingly replacing traditional methods of sharing community information and engaging individuals in civic activities. Yet, much is still unknown globally about the extent to which individuals are adopting new technologies for civic communication purposes. Methods This survey of Indian adults investigates the relationship between media use, online activity, media literacy, and civic engagement, offering insights into how digital technology shapes civic participation in one of the world's largest countries. Participants self-reported the amount of traditional, digital, and social media they access. Additionally, they self-reported their online consumption and production of digital content, ability to critically assess media information, and types of civic activities. Results Findings build on previous work among Western populations showing that, online interactions can predict higher civic engagement. However, this Indian population appears less interested in media literacy behaviors compared to their counterparts elsewhere. Traditional news organization access continues to predict higher levels of civic engagement, while more entertainment-based platforms do not. Discussion In a study spanning multiple languages in a large and complex democratic environment, results confirm previous scholarship on the interrelationship between news consumption in civic engagement. Additionally, these findings highlight the value of critical assessment of media content, including both a comfort with navigating interactive elements of digital technology and critically evaluating the information found online, in strengthening civic engagement.
