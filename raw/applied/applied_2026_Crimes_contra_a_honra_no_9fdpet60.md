# Crimes contra a honra no contexto das relações virtuais

**저자**: Isabela Rossini Ferreira; Leticya Regina de Azevedo Mariano; Melissa Mendonça Proença; Yuri Yudi Yoscimoto
**출처**: Revista fisio&terapia., Vol.30 No.162, pp.01-31

**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

This article aims to analyze crimes against honor committed in virtual environments, considering the transformations brought about by the development of the internet and the increasing use of social networks and other digital platforms as means of communication. The research addresses the main forms of offenses against honor committed in cyberspace and examines how the Brazilian legal system protects victims of such conduct. In this context, the study seeks to understand the characteristics of crimes committed through digital means, examine the legal mechanisms available for their repression, and identify the challenges arising from the application of criminal law to new forms of interaction enabled by technology. Particular attention is given to the crimes of calumny, defamation, and insult, as provided for under Brazilian Criminal Law, analyzing their legal characterization when offensive statements are made through the internet. The methodology adopted consists of bibliographic and documentary research based on legislation, legal doctrine, case law, and scientific studies related to the subject. The analysis demonstrates that the digital environment has significantly increased the potential dissemination of offenses against honor, since content that would previously have been restricted to a small number of individuals can now rapidly reach a large audience. Posts, comments, messages, and other content shared on digital platforms may remain accessible, be reproduced, and circulate among different audiences, thereby increasing the consequences of the conduct. Furthermore, the speed at which information circulates and the possibility of successive sharing make it more difficult to contain the harm caused to the victim’s reputation, dignity, and social standing. Therefore, although crimes against honor did not originate with the internet, the virtual environment has significantly transformed the scope and repercussions of such conduct, requiring an interpretation of criminal legislation that takes into account the particularities of digital relationships. It is concluded that the protection of honor in virtual environments depends both on the proper application of existing criminal provisions and on an understanding of the new ways in which offenses may be produced, disseminated, and amplified by communication technologies.
