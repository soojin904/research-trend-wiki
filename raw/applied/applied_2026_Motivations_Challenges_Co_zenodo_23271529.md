## 초록

This qualitative study employed Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis (IPA) as its research design and centered on eight mathematics educators from the Negros Island Region in the Philippines. These educators were officially enrolled in master's or doctoral programs at Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) during the academic year 2024-2025. The researcher employed purposive-convenience sampling, selecting readily accessible mathematics educators who also met specific criteria: they had conducted at least one research study, had presented a study, and had taught in a public high school for at least two years. The study sought to understand their lived experiences related to research engagement, using in-depth interviews to capture their motivations, challenges, coping mechanisms, perceived benefits, and insights. The mathematics educators' primary motivator stemmed from a genuine desire to improve their learners' lives, focusing on strategies to enhance comprehension, reduce anxiety, and promote equity. However, these educators faced considerable challenges, including time constraints due to heavy workloads and limited resources, particularly in public schools and part-time positions. To overcome these obstacles, they demonstrated resourcefulness and relied on collaboration, sharing ideas and leveraging networks for support. Despite the hardships, research engagement brought significant rewards, most notably witnessing learners' "lightbulb moments" and experiencing professional growth. Ultimately, the study highlighted the dedication and resilience of these educators, emphasizing the urgent need for institutional support to foster a research culture that empowers them to advance mathematics education in the Philippines.
