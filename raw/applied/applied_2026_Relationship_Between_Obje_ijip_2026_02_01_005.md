# Relationship Between Objectified Body Consciousness and Frustration Intolerance Among Young Adult Male Gym Participants in Kerala

**저자**: Adhisree P

## 초록

The present study examined the relationship between body consciousness and frustration among individuals engaged in physical fitness activities. A quantitative correlational research design was employed to investigate the psychological experiences associated with fitness participation. The sample consisted of 105 male participants aged 18–30 years who regularly engaged in gym-based fitness activities. Data were collected using the Objectified Body Consciousness Scale (OBCS) and the Frustration Discomfort Scale (FDS). Statistical analyses included descriptive statistics, independent samples t-tests, Pearson’s correlation. Pearson correlation analysis revealed a weak but significant positive relationship between body consciousness and frustration (r=.21, p<.05). However, emotional distress, frustration, and entitlement were positively associated with one another. Body perception demonstrated a negative relationship with emotional distress, indicating that individuals with more positive body perceptions experienced lower levels of emotional strain. The results suggest that emotional and cognitive factors play an important role in shaping psychological experiences within fitness settings. The findings highlight the importance of addressing psychological well-being alongside physical fitness in exercise settings.The study contributes to a better understanding of body-related perceptions and emotional well-being among young adults engaged in regular physical fitness activities and highlights the importance of promoting positive body perception to support psychological well-being.
