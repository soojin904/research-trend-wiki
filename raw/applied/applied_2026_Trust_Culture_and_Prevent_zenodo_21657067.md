# Trust, Culture, and Preventive Health: Why African Migrants in Europe Disengage from Health Services: A Narrative Review

**저자**: Ima Frankson Uso
**출처**: Zenodo (CERN European Organization for Nuclear Research)
**발행일**: 2026-07-29

**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

African migrant communities in Europe consistently demonstrate lower engagement with preventive health services, including vaccination, cancer screening, and health promotion programmes, compared to host-country populations. Existing scholarship has disproportionately attributed this disparity to structural barriers such as insurance gaps, documentation status, and language proficiency. This narrative review argues that such an explanatory framework is insufficient and that the sociocultural and communicative dimensions of preventive health disengagement have been systematically underweighted in the European public health literature. Drawing on a narrative synthesis of 42 peer-reviewed studies and policy reports published between 2020 and 2026, this paper examines three interrelated explanatory domains: (a) the role of cultural and religious belief systems in shaping health risk perception and illness interpretation among African migrants; (b) the influence of institutional trust, shaped by prior healthcare experiences and encounters with racism, on willingness to engage with European health services; and (c) the function of informal communication ecologies, including diaspora social networks and faith communities, as mediators of health information and decision-making. The paper concludes with evidence-based recommendations for the design of culturally responsive, community-informed public health interventions.
