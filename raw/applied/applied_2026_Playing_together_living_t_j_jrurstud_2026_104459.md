# Playing together, living together: Community-based music learning as a catalyst for rural wellbeing

**저자**: Arianna Ranieri; Zack Moir
**출처**: Journal of Rural Studies, Vol.128, pp.104459-104459
**발행일**: 2026-10-11
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrurstud.2026.104459
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

This article explores how participatory instrumental music tuition can strengthen wellbeing, confidence, and social cohesion for adults living in rural Scotland. Focusing on the town of Millport, Isle of Cumbrae, the study was conducted as part of the Community Instrumental Music Service for Rural Scotland (CIMSRS), an initiative designed to address barriers to adult music learning caused by isolation, limited provision, and economic constraints. Nineteen adults aged between 25 and 75 took part in weekly group violin and viola sessions, culminating in a community performance. Through individual interviews, group discussions, and reflective questionnaires, participants described their idiosyncratic understanding of the challenges of island life, including restricted transport links, economic precarity, and feelings of invisibility in policy discourse. They also describe how these intersected with age-related barriers to musical learning and participation and share insights into the effectiveness of CIMSRS in combatting some of these issues. Engagement with CIMSRS provided a rare opportunity to build confidence, rediscover creativity, and develop new social ties across generations. Participants reported increased wellbeing, purpose, and belonging through “accidental learning” in an empathetic, informal environment that encouraged agency rather than instruction. The findings illustrate how accessible, community-led music provision can become a lifeline for rural adults, transforming isolation into connection and nurturing sustainable, self-organizing networks of cultural participation. The study suggests that meaningful rural cultural provision must move beyond the importation of instructor-driven urban models, instead supporting locally grounded, relational practices that recognize the lived realities of rural adulthood in any given location and context.
