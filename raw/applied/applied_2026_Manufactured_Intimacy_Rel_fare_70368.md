# Manufactured Intimacy: Relational Ecology and the Case of Married at First Sight

**저자**: Neil Wilkie
**출처**: Family Relations
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1111/fare.70368
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

ABSTRACT Objective To develop a conceptual explanation for why the Married at First Sight (MAFS) television format reports relationship retention rates markedly below other documented pathways to partnership. Background Longitudinal evidence indicates that, once the era of meeting is controlled for, the pathway of formation has little independent effect on long‐term outcomes. MAFS is a striking exception, with publicly reported retention well below this pattern, unexplained by existing accounts. Methods This conceptual paper synthesises longitudinal panel data on couple formation, attachment, interpersonal‐neurobiological, and phenomenological literatures, alongside a close analysis of the MAFS format as an illustrative case; no primary data were collected. Results We propose that partnership durability depends substantially on relational ecology—the network of witnessing, accountable relationships holding a couple from formation into ordinary partnership—and argue MAFS may engineer an artificial ecology designed to dissolve, producing the institutional‐blessing dynamic. Conclusion Pathway of formation appears less consequential for relationship durability than the relational ecology surrounding a couple, a hypothesis illustrated through, but not established by, the MAFS case. Implications or Recommendations Findings point to the clinical value of sustained, two‐clinician relational support offered earlier in partnership, and highlight a regulatory gap facing quasi‐clinical televised matchmaking formats.
