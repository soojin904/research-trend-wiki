## 초록

Fungus-fungivore associations are central to forest ecosystem functioning where fungi mediate decomposition, provide shelter and nutrition for insects, whereas insects sporocarp-feeding and indirect spore dispersal in turn alter the fungal community assembly. Understanding fungus-fungivore associations based on meta-analysis is at the moment challenging because of unclear species taxonomy, difficulty in identifing samples based on historical descriptions, scarcity of genetic information, and over-simplified interaction records. Meta-analysis is also challenging because of different sampling strategies and definitions of biotic interaction. Here we propose a standardized workflow by integrating morphological identification, molecular data, and host records using a diverse lineage of Taiwanese fungivorous beetles (Erotylinae) as a model group. We found out that genetic divergence was not always accompanied by geographical isolation and morphological differences, i.e., many cryptic lineages. Most Erotylinae were generically monophagous. Further comparative phylogenetic analyses showed that multiple beetle species feed on the same fungal genus, which suggests repeated host shifts rather than co-diversification with the hosts. Our results demonstrate the importance of standardized procedures in studying species-species associations. For example, the interaction records that provide the host spectrum of a particular species can be used for testing the host specializations. Furthermore, the impacts of fungus diversity on beetle diversification can be examined.
