# Wanting more: adolescents' expectations for social media responses from peers

**저자**: Celina K. Bowman‐Smith; Elizabeth S. Nilsen
**출처**: Frontiers in Developmental Psychology, Vol.4
**발행일**: 2026-07-27
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fdpys.2026.1796593
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

Introduction Associations between adolescents' social media use and wellbeing vary widely across studies, with motivation for social media use explaining some of the variability. However, little is known about adolescents' consideration of their motivations during the process of creating social media posts. Methods Addressing this gap, we employed an experimental design wherein adolescents (13–16; N = 103) participated in a simulated Instagram task where they created posts and indicated how much they hoped to receive engagement, advice, support, or entertainment. Later, other adolescents viewed posts and rated what the original poster had hoped to receive. Additionally, adolescents completed measures of their socio-emotional characteristics. Together, we were able to capture the responses adolescents hoped to receive from others while posting messages, the degree to which other adolescents can detect the posters' wanted responses, and whether adolescent characteristics related to the types of responses posters hoped to receive. Results Results found that viewers' ratings of what posters hoped to receive correlated with what the posters themselves reported wanting (most strongly for support and advice). However, the magnitude of ratings differed with posters reporting that they hoped for more of the responses (engagement, advice, and support, entertainment) than the viewers perceived them to want. Adolescent characteristics, including empathy, mood, emotion regulation, peer relationships, as well as frequency of social media use, related to the responses they hoped to receive from others. Discussion Findings suggest a mismatch between social media posters' motivation and online viewers' perceptions, with variability in motivations partially explained by adolescents' socio-emotional characteristics.
