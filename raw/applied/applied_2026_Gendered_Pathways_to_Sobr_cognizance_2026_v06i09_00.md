# Gendered Pathways to Sobriety: A Conceptual Framework of Group Cohesion in Stimulant Use Disorder Treatment

## 초록

Stimulant use disorder (SUD) occupies a uniquely difficult position within addiction medicine: unlike opioid and alcohol use disorders, it has no regulator-approved pharmacotherapy, leaving psychosocial intervention as the primary rather than the adjunctive line of treatment. Because the majority of that intervention is delivered in groups, group cohesion is not a peripheral process variable in stimulant recovery but a central therapeutic mechanism. Yet the cohesion literature, and the group therapy literature more broadly, has largely treated the patient as a monolith. This paper argues that this assumption is untenable. Drawing together three literatures that rarely speak to one another — Yalom’s theory of therapeutic factors, the empirical literature on sex and gender differences in stimulant use trajectories, and the evidence base for gender-responsive addiction treatment — we advance a conceptual framework in which cohesion is a common therapeutic mechanism that is constituted differently, and works through different mediating pathways, for men and for women. We propose that women derive therapeutic benefit from cohesion principally through affective safety, reciprocal disclosure and the repair of relational schemas damaged by interpersonal trauma, whereas men derive benefit principally through instrumental bonding, structured peer accountability and the normative reframing of vulnerability. We formalise this as five testable propositions and a moderated-mediation model linking gendered vulnerability to sobriety outcomes via cohesion subtype and retention. We argue that the segregated-versus-integrated debate is a false binary and propose phased and process-matched alternatives. Finally, we identify the methodological deficiencies — chiefly the failure to disaggregate by sex, to model group-level nesting and to study stimulant-specific cohorts — that currently prevent the field from testing these claims.
