# The Impact of Collaborative Network Characteristics on the Knowledge Recombination Diversity of Hydrogen Energy Patent Applicants

**저자**: Hyun Sil Moon; Hyea Kyeong Kim; Hee Jin Im; Il Young Choi
**출처**: Asia-pacific Journal of Convergent Research Interchange, Vol.12 No.9, pp.201-216
**발행일**: 2026-09-28
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.47116/apjcri.2026.09.14
**수집 키워드**: social network

## 초록

This study investigates the multidimensional mechanisms through which collaborative network characteristics influence the knowledge recombination diversity of patent applicants in the hydrogen energy sector, empirically analyzing how network resources affect innovation potential in a technological convergence environment.To this end, a panel dataset comprising 325 observations of domestic corporate and institutional applicants who applied for hydrogen energy patents between 2019 and 2024 was established.Using co-applicant information, annual collaboration networks were constructed to calculate four types of network centrality: degree, betweenness, closeness, and eigenvector centrality.The dependent variable, knowledge recombination diversity, was quantified using an entropy index based on the first four digits of International Patent Classification (IPC) codes.Based on the Hausman test results, two-way panel fixed-effects regression analyses were primarily conducted, and a dynamic robustness check incorporating a t-1 lagged variable was integrated to enhance methodological rigor.The empirical results demonstrate that betweenness centrality exerts a consistently significant positive impact on knowledge recombination diversity across both static and dynamic models.This proves that serving as a broker across structural holes to bridge disconnected technological groups is a critical driver for absorbing heterogeneous external knowledge and expanding technological boundaries.Conversely, degree centrality showed a significant positive effect only in the static model and lost its significance in the dynamic model, revealing the limitations of a volumeoriented expansion strategy focused solely on the number of direct partners.Furthermore, closeness centrality exhibited a significant negative effect in the dynamic lagging model, providing empirical evidence for the "paradox of speed" where overly efficient information access accelerates knowledge
