# Intimate dispossession: forced relocation and the gendered reorganization of social reproduction in Jakarta

## 초록

Slum clearance remains a central feature of state-led urban development across rapidly urbanising contexts in the Global South, yet its consequences for everyday life remain unevenly understood. Drawing on social reproduction theory and the concept of depletion, this paper examines the gendered consequences of forced relocation from informal settlement (kampungs) to social housing (rusunawa) in Jakarta, Indonesia. Based on ethnographic findings that center women’s experiences, it demonstrates that forced relocation depletes women by dismantling the socio-spatial infrastructure of care in kampungs that sustains social reproduction in informal settlements. Three mechanisms through which this depletion operates are identified: the accumulation of debt and dispossession; disruption to quotidian practices; and physical and mental exhaustion. I argue that these mechanisms constitute a gendered, intimate, quiet, and embodied form of dispossession, as women disproportionately bear the multifaceted costs of adjusting to social housing that renders domestic and care work more time-intensive, more precarious, and more isolating. This analysis foregrounds homes and women’s bodies as additional sites of extraction that are routinely overlooked in dominant accounts of urban restructuring. These dynamics suggest that contemporary urban transformation in Southern cities is underpinned not only by the reconfiguration of land and infrastructure, but also by the systematic reorganization of social reproduction.
