# Moving beyond constrained settings: Health and network dynamics among middle-aged and older adults in voluntary clubs
**제목(한글)**: 제약된 환경을 넘어서: 자발적 클럽 내 중장년 및 노인의 건강과 네트워크 역학

**저자**: Amelie Reiner; James Moody
**출처**: Network Science, Vol.13
**발행일**: 2025-01-01
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1017/nws.2025.10011

## 한국어 요약

**연구질문**: 완전히 자발적인 환경(voluntary clubs)에서 건강과 사회 네트워크가 어떻게 상호 영향을 미치는가, 그리고 기존의 제약된 환경에서 관찰된 패턴이 이 맥락에서도 나타나는가?

**방법론**:
- 독일 노르트라인-베스트팔렌 소재 자발적 클럽 2곳의 3파 종단 전체 네트워크 데이터 분석 (N=102, 평균 54세)
- 확률적 행위자 지향 모델(SAOM)로 건강 기반 선택 및 영향 효과 구분
- 자기평가, 정신건강, 신체건강 세 가지 건강 지표 분석

**주요 결과**:
- 어떤 건강 지표에서도 또래 영향(peer influence) 증거 없음
- 건강이 나쁜 사람이 관계를 회피하는 사회적 고립 패턴은 미약하거나 부재
- 오히려 건강이 나쁜 사람들이 더 적극적으로 타인을 지명하며 사회적 연결을 보상 전략으로 사용
- 자발적 맥락의 네트워크-건강 역학이 기존 가정과 다름을 입증

## 초록 (원문)

Abstract Social networks influence health outcomes, yet declining health can also reshape social ties. While prior research has focused on constrained settings, the impact of health on social networks in fully voluntary contexts remains underexplored. This study examines the reciprocal relationship between health and social networks in voluntary settings, assessing whether previously observed patterns persist. We analyzed three-wave longitudinal whole network data from two voluntary clubs ( N = 102, mean age = 54 years) in North-Rhine Westphalia, Germany, using Stochastic Actor-Oriented Models to distinguish between selection and influence effects across self-rated, mental, and physical health measures. Our analyses suggest diverging patterns observed in more constrained settings. We found no evidence of peer influence on health across any measures. While self-rated health showed some evidence of selection effects, social avoidance was limited to individuals with poor physical health. Notably, we found no evidence of withdrawal; instead, individuals with poorer health were more likely to nominate others in the network, suggesting they actively sought social connections as a compensatory strategy. These findings challenge existing assumptions about health-based network dynamics, emphasizing the need to reconsider how social networks function in voluntary contexts. Future research should explore how the degree of setting constraints shape health-related network dynamics.
