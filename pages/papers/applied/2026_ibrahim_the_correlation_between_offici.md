# The Correlation Between Official Statements by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Changes in the Foreign Investment Risk Index

**저자**: Riza Ibrahim; Mugi Lestari
**출처**: International Journal of Linguistics Communication and Broadcasting, Vol.4, pp.226-239
**발행일**: 2026-09-30
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.46336/ijlcb.v4i3.366

## 초록 (원문)

Foreign investment decisions are shaped not only by economic fundamentals but also by political information that alters perceptions of uncertainty and future country conditions. This study investigates whether the substantive informational content of official diplomatic statements is systematically associated with changes in foreign investment risk. A quantitative longitudinal design was employed, integrating Natural Language Processing, event-window analysis, and two-way fixed-effects panel regression across 1,065 observations. Official statements issued by ministries of foreign affairs were transformed into five textual indicators: sentiment, risk intensity, geopolitical content, economic cooperation, and security content, and subsequently matched with investment-risk changes and macroeconomic and global controls. The results demonstrate that substantive communication characteristics provide greater explanatory value than statement frequency. Risk intensity, geopolitical content, and security content are positively and significantly associated with investment-risk change, whereas economic-cooperation content exhibits a significant negative association. Sentiment remains comparatively weak and theoretically ambiguous, indicating that generic linguistic polarity cannot adequately capture the economically consequential signals embedded in strategically framed diplomatic discourse. Robustness analyses across alternative estimators, event windows, lagged specifications, and placebo tests support the stability of the principal findings. The study contributes to political communication and international political economy by conceptualizing official diplomatic discourse as a multidimensional information channel through which governments transmit economically relevant signals. Methodologically, it demonstrates the value of integrating computational text analysis with longitudinal econometric identification to examine how institutional communication becomes associated with investor risk assessments.
