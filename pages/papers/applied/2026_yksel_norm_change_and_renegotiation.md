# Norm Change and Renegotiation in Higher Education in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: A Conceptual Review from the Perspective of Social Network Analysis

**저자**: Akça Okan Yüksel
**출처**: IntechOpen eBooks, Vol.None
**발행일**: 2026-10-12
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.5772/intechopen.1018289

## 초록 (원문)

The rapid integration of generative artificial intelligence (AI) into higher education has disrupted the invisible normative infrastructure of universities at multiple points simultaneously. Unlike previous technological transitions, AI enters the higher education system concurrently through three interconnected levels: the student level, where the meaning of assignments, exams, and individual work is renegotiated; the faculty level, where authorship, peer review, course preparation, and assessment practices are reshaped; and the institutional level, where academic integrity policies, accreditation processes, and relations of trust with publishers are restructured. This simultaneous and systemic disruption produces a relational normative phenomenon that individualistic accounts of AI usage fail to adequately capture. This chapter develops a conceptual framework by bringing together social norm theory in the tradition of Bicchieri and Coleman, the structural social network analysis of Granovetter, Burt, Valente, and Centola, and the sociology of higher education following Bourdieu and Bernstein. It identifies five sets of norms undergoing simultaneous transformation (authorship and ownership of work, assessment and its meaning, pedagogical authority, knowledge ownership, and academic productivity expectations) and analyzes how four properties of academic networks (density, brokerage, centrality, and boundary permeability) shape the speed, direction, and stability of normative change. The primary contribution is a systemic reading that traces AI-induced norm flows vertically across student, faculty, and institutional levels. Here, AI is read not as a novel tool but as a relational actor circulating within academic networks, accelerating certain norms, eroding others, and triggering the emergence of new normative consensuses.
