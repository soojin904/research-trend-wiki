# Can Machines Think Like Humans: A Behavioral Evaluation of LLM Agents in Dictator Games

## 한국어 요약

**연구질문**: LLM 에이전트들이 경제학적 독재자 게임(Dictator Games)에서 보이는 친사회적 및 이타적 행동은 어떻게 결정되며, 인간의 행동 패턴과 정량적으로 정렬되는가?

**방법론**:
- 다양한 성격(Persona) 프롬프트 및 실험 설정을 적용
- 여러 LLM 제품군을 대상으로 독재자 게임 내 분배 결정 금액과 의사결정 이유 텍스트를 추출해 인간의 행동 지표와 대조 분석함

**주요 결과**:
- LLM에 단순히 인간의 자아 페르소나를 매핑하는 것만으로는 인간다운 친사회적 행태가 유도되지 않음을 밝힘
- 프롬프트 문체와 아키텍처에 따른 이타적 행동 변동성이 극도로 심해 체계적 정렬 가이드라인의 시급성을 규명함


## 초록 (원문)



Abstract As large language model (LLM)-based agents increasingly engage with human society, how well do we understand their prosocial behaviors? We (1) investigate how LLM agents’ prosocial behaviors can be induced by different personas and benchmarked against human behaviors and (2) introduce a social science approach to evaluate LLM agents’ decision-making. We explored how different personas and experimental framings affect these AI agents’ altruistic behavior in dictator games and compared their behaviors within the same LLM family, across various families, and with human behaviors. The findings reveal that merely assigning a human-like identity to LLMs does not produce human-like behaviors. They suggest that LLM agents’ reasoning does not consistently exhibit textual markers of human decision-making in dictator games and that their alignment with human behavior varies substantially across model architectures and prompt formulations; even worse, such dependence does not follow a clear pattern. As society increasingly integrates machine intelligence, “prosocial AI” emerges as a promising and urgent research direction in philanthropic studies.
