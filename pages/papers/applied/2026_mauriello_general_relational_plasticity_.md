## 초록 (원문)

Title: General Relational Plasticity Theory (TRP): A Substrate-Invariant Framework for Critical State Transitions and Adaptive Systems Abstract: Complex adaptive systems—ranging from neural networks and social structures to malignant cellular tissues—exhibit a fundamental tension between structural stability and adaptive plasticity. This paper introduces the General Relational Plasticity Theory (TRP), a novel substrate-invariant mathematical framework designed to model, predict, and navigate critical state transitions. We formalize TRP through a relational matrix ($W$), a constraint function ($C$), a plasticity capacity ($\Pi$), and a constraint-gated anchoring factor modulated by a critical threshold ($C_{min}$). The core mechanism demonstrates that when external or internal perturbations drive constraints below $C_{min}$, the system undergoes a rapid collapse of historical anchoring, opening a quantifiable "window of plasticity" that enables structural reorganization. We validate the mathematical core of the theory through computational simulations across three distinct domains: neural stability, social network polarization dynamics, and oncological state shifts (specifically epithelial-mesenchymal transition in late-stage cancer). The results confirm that TRP successfully captures non-linear critical transitions, providing a unified predictive architecture for anticipating system failure, managing phase changes, and designing targeted precision interventions. Keywords: General Relational Plasticity Theory, Substrate-Invariant Dynamics, Critical Thresholds, Constraint-Gated Plasticity, Complex Systems, Predictive Modeling.
