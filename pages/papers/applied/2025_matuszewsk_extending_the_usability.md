# Extending the Usability of Digital Music Libraries with Analytical Interfaces Case Study – traditional music collections

**저자**: Anna Maria Matuszewska
**출처**: , Vol.None, pp.336-339
**발행일**: 2025-12-15
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1109/jcdl67857.2025.00060

## 초록 (원문)

This paper presents a component of a broader project aimed at developing an interactive analytical interface for the exploration of traditional music corpora. Implemented as a Shiny application for R, the platform integrates statistical music analysis with sentiment polarity and emotion recognition, enabling scholars to study relationships between melodic, rhythmic, and textual features. The case study focuses on the DEUTSCHL subset of the Essen Folksong Collection, comprising over 4,000 melodies from German-speaking regions alongside a newly digitized and standardized corpus of lyrics. The interface is organized into interconnected dashboards that support two modes of interaction: distant reading, for statistical comparison of corpora, subcorpora, and user-defined sets; and close reading, for detailed visualizations of individual melodies and their associated texts. Synchronized visualizations — including rhythm course plots and sentiment polarity diagrams — allow dynamic filtering by metadata, musical parameters, or lyrical content. Emotion-based analysis, largely independent of language, further opens possibilities for comparative research across culturally diverse collections. Designed for accessibility, the system enables scholars without programming expertise to engage deeply with digitized sources. Grounded in UX research with 47 musicologists and five music theorists, the project demonstrates how integrating text analysis into music digital libraries can enhance search, navigation, and classification, transforming repositories into active research environments that foster discovery, interpretation, and cross-disciplinary inquiry.
