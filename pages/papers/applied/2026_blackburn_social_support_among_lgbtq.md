# Social Support among LGBTQ, Cisgender, and Heterosexual Undergraduate Students: An Ego-Centric Social Network Study

**저자**: Allyson M. Blackburn; Nathan Todd; Nicole E. Allen
**출처**: , Vol.None
**발행일**: 2026-10-09
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/udxmy_v1

## 초록 (원문)

While the population and acceptance of LGBTQ people are growing, there are persistent mental health disparities for LGBTQ individuals, especially for emerging adults. Social support is critical for the promotion of well-being. Yet, who receives support and the nature of the support provided can vary based on social identity, including sexual orientation and gender identity. In this study, we demonstrate the benefit of using ego-centric social network analysis (ESNA) to advance research about emotional support for sexual minorities. Using a sample of 1,215 undergraduate students (253 sexual minorities) from a large Midwestern university, we show that sexual minorities reported a greater proportion of LGBTQ people yet a lower proportion of family in their social network. We also show that sexual minorities reported that they were less likely to receive support but were more likely to be nominated by both heterosexuals and sexual minorities as network members who provide support. Finally, sexual minority participants relied more on friends than parents for emotional support, whereas the opposite was true for heterosexual students. These (and other) findings show how ESNA allows for testing hypotheses that capture the interconnected context of relationships. We discuss how findings advance the literature along with limitations and implications. Overall, we show that ESNA is a valuable tool to use to understand social networks as a critical context for development and well-being.
