## 초록 (원문)

This chapter examines how computer-assisted analytical techniques have enabled scholars to ask new questions of text-based cultural materials, framing the tension between computational and traditional modes of reading as a false opposition. The chapter introduces distant reading as a broad spectrum of methods, from basic word frequency analysis to advanced natural language processing and machine learning, and explains how these approaches complement rather than replace close reading. It surveys major tools and methods used in computational text analysis, discussing landmark studies by scholars such as Matthew Jockers and Ted Underwood. The chapter also looks ahead to the impact of large language models on the future of computation for criticism, considering how artificial intelligence may reshape the relationship between quantitative evidence and humanistic interpretation.
