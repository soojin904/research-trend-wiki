# Comparative Textual Analysis of Global News Media: A Web Scraping and NLP Approach to Content Patterns Across Five Major News Outlets

**저자**: Sareena Bilal
**출처**: Figshare, Vol.None
**발행일**: 2026-09-13
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.33691180.v1

## 초록 (원문)

This study presents a comprehensive comparative analysis of textual content patterns across five major international news organizations: BBC, CNN, Al Jazeera, The Guardian, and The New York Times. Using automated web scraping techniques, we collected 500 recent news articles from each outlet's RSS feed, totaling 2,500 articles. Natural Language Processing (NLP) methods including text preprocessing, TF-IDF vectorization, and exploratory data analysis were applied to identify linguistic patterns, content focus areas, and editorial differences. Class distribution analysis revealed varying content categorization patterns across outlets, with Al Jazeera showing a 60-40 split between categories, BBC demonstrating a 58-42 distribution, CNN exhibiting a 64-36 ratio, The New York Times showing a 60-40 split, and The Guardian displaying a 54-46 balance. Word cloud visualizations uncovered distinct thematic emphases: Al Jazeera focused on conflict-related terminology ("military," "attack," "Israel," "Palestinian"), BBC emphasized political and economic terms ("year," "new," "say," "Russian"), CNN highlighted governmental and political discourse ("elect," "country," "nation," "campaign"), The New York Times centered on political figures and processes ("president," "vice," "group," "editorial"), and The Guardian featured social and cultural topics ("people," "read," "continue," "strong"). This research demonstrates the utility of automated text analysis for media bias detection and content characterization, providing a methodological framework for large-scale comparative media studies.
