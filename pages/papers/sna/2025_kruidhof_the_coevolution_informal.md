# The co-evolution of informal social status and gossip in workplace social networks
**제목(한글)**: 직장 소셜 네트워크에서 비공식 사회적 지위와 가십의 공진화

**저자**: Emily Kruidhof; Rense Corten; Lea Ellwardt; Rafael Wittek
**출처**: Social Networks, Vol.84, pp.9–26
**발행일**: 2025-08-11
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socnet.2025.07.006


## 한국어 요약

**연구질문**: 직장에서 비공식 사회적 지위와 가십 삼자 관계(송신자, 수신자, 대상)가 서로 어떻게 공진화하는가?

**방법론**:
- 네덜란드 어린이집 직원 대상 3파 종단 사회 네트워크 연구
- 확률적 행위자 지향 모델(SAOM)을 이용한 공진화 분석
- 사회적 유대 관점(social bonding) vs. 사회적 자본 관점(social capital) 가설 검증

**주요 결과**:
- 비공식 지위와 가십 수신자 역할이 공진화하는 것으로 확인
- 낮은 지위의 직원이 가십을 더 많이 수신하며, 이를 통해 지위가 향상됨
- 가십 전송과 가십 대상이 되는 것은 비공식 지위에 영향을 미치지 않음
- 시간이 지남에 따라 직원의 비공식 지위가 안정되는 균형 상태 출현 가능성 시사

## 초록 (원문)

This study examines the co-evolution of informal social status and the three positions in a gossip triad – gossip senders, receivers, and objects – in the workplace. Two different social mechanisms are proposed to explain these interrelationships, suggesting relationships between gossip and informal social status in the opposite direction. First, the social bonding perspective suggests that gossip bonds between actors in a gossip triad shape their informal social status. Second, the social capital perspective indicates that employees’ informal social status leads to their position within gossip triads. The hypotheses are tested in a three-wave social network study among employees in a Dutch childcare organization. Results of stochastic actor-oriented models indicate a co-evolution between informal social status and the receiver’s role in a gossip triad, but not with the roles of sender and object. Contrary to what the social capital perspective predicts, employees’ informal social status negatively affects receiving gossip over time. In line with the social bonding perspective, receiving gossip positively affects informal social status over time. The co-evolution process suggests that over time, an equilibrium may emerge where an employee’s informal social status stabilizes at a point where enough gossip is received to sustain their social position. We conclude that the previously often neglected receivers of third-party information should be considered when examining the dynamics of workplace gossip. • We investigate the co-evolution of informal social status and gossip. • Informal social status and receiving gossip co-evolve. • Lower-status employees receive more gossip, which enhances their status over time. • Sending gossip and being the object of gossip does not affect one’s informal social status, and vice versa.
